Policy & Regulation

Social housing energy upgrades pass 50,000 properties
Source: DCCEEW / Assistant Minister Josh Wilson | Date: 2026-10-19
URL：Original source
Key point: The government reported on 19 October that more than 50,000 social housing properties had received upgrades under the Social Housing Energy Performance Initiative (SHEPI), halfway towards its target of more than 100,000. Delivery to date includes 3,500 solar PV systems, 6,000 hot-water heat pumps and 6,000 reverse-cycle air conditioners. This is a progress milestone in the existing programme.
Regional representatives seek stronger local benefits from NSW renewable-energy zones
Source: RE-Alliance / Medianet | Date: 2026-10-19
URL：Original source
Key point: In a 19 October release, RE-Alliance described meetings held on 14–15 October between NSW MPs and representatives of three renewable-energy zones. Delegates sought better access to project contracts, community participation and lasting local infrastructure; the organisation also called for stronger EnergyCo coordination and recurrent council funding. These are advocacy proposals ahead of the NSW Government’s inquiry response, due on 29 October.

Company & Project Developments

Fortescue reports first sales from Brisbane copper-cleaning demonstration
Source: Fortescue | Date: 2026-10-19
URL：Original source
Key point: Fortescue said on 19 October that it had sold more than 40 tonnes of copper concentrate treated at its Brisbane demonstration plant using the Toowong Process. The company reported arsenic below 0.25% in the first product, from feedstock containing up to 7%. The technology could support copper supply for renewable projects and electricity networks; process optimisation and commercial engineering remain under way.

Infrastructure & Transmission

Powerlink draft reduces ten-year synchronous-condenser requirement from nine to five
Source: Powerlink Queensland | Date: 2026-10-19
URL：Original source · Consultation page
Key point: Powerlink’s 19 October draft retains synchronous condensers as the preferred system-strength option after revised coal-retirement assumptions. It proposes two Central Queensland units by March 2029, two more by August 2029 and a fifth by January 2031; the fifth remains uncommitted to allow credible non-network alternatives. Submissions close on 30 November, with a final statement due by 9 December. The assessment remains a draft.
