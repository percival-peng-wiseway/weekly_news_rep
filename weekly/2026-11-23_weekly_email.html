气候与电力路径

  • 年度气候声明与 2026 年排放预测：基准情形下 2030 年 NEM 可再生发电占 84%
    来源：DCCEEW；Climate Change Authority；ABC News · 2026-11-26 · 核验：已核验 · 排放预测 · 预测PDF · 年度声明 · CCA · ABC
    要点：DCCEEW 于 11 月 26 日发布第五份年度气候变化声明和 2026 年排放预测，Climate Change Authority 同日发布年度进展建议。声明称全国排放减少 620 万吨，CCA 称并网可再生电力目前约 44%；ABC 报道国家电力市场可再生发电占 45.9%，部门摘要写明了 620 万吨，没有重复列出 45.9%。预测的基准情形假设现行政策落实，其中包括 2030 年全国并网可再生发电达到 82%，并假设已宣布的大型输电按期投运；在该假设下，国家电力市场可再生发电占比 2030 年升至 84%、2040 年升至 92%，2030 年排放比 2005 年低 42%、比 2021–2030 年排放预算高 0.3%，按现行政策 2035 年预计比 2005 年低 48%，目标则是低 62%–70%；这些是情景结果，不是已经实现的发电份额。

    来源：Clean Energy Regulator；RenewEconomy · 2026-11-26 · 核验：已核验 · CER季报 · RenewEconomy
    要点：清洁能源监管机构 11 月 26 日发布 2026 年 9 月季度的完整《季度碳市场报告》：三季度 2.1 GW 大型可再生发电达到最终投资决定，其中风电 1.1 GW，为该机构有记录以来的单季最高，光伏 1.0 GW。前三季度累计 5.0 GW，已经超过 2018 年创下的全年纪录 4.9 GW，报告称截至 11 月中旬年内累计进一步升至 5.5 GW。截至三季度末，达到最终投资决定的发电容量中约 52% 获得容量投资计划支持，2026–2027 年最终投资决定预测的下限从 6 GW 上调后区间为 8–16 GW，储能达到最终投资决定的容量为三季度 13.8 GWh、2026 年累计 20.0 GWh；大型可再生能源目标下的 2026 年审批预测从 3.5–4.5 GW 下调为 2.6–3.4 GW，报告引述 AEMO 称，电网级电池参与增加加上电网需求下降，使国家电力市场批发电价比 2025 年三季度低 27%。

AU Renewable Energy Weekly: 23 Nov – 27 Nov 2026

Policy & Regulation

  • Industry working group recommends three standard electricity contracts for the proposed ESEM
    Source: ASL · 2026-11-23 · Verification: Verified · ASL release
    Key point: On 23 November the industry working group convened by DCCEEW and ASL, with the AER as an observer, published a stage-one report recommending three long-term standard contracts for the proposed Electricity Services Entry Mechanism: a Regional Reference PPA for bulk energy, a Daily Dynamic Block Spread swap for shaping, and a $600/MWh cap for firming. Energy ministers have agreed in principle, except Queensland, to the core recommendations of the 2025 NEM Review, including an ESEM. Long-form contracts, ministerial decisions and designation by the ESEM administrator are still outstanding; this is a design recommendation, not an open tender.
  • Commonwealth and NSW open consultation on an approval bilateral for solar and batteries, closing 11 January
    Source: Federal environment minister; DCCEEW · 2026-11-26 · Verification: Verified · Joint release · DCCEEW page
    Key point: On 26 November, Environment and Water Minister Murray Watt, NSW Minister Penny Sharpe and NSW Minister Paul Scully opened consultation on a proposed pilot approval bilateral agreement. The release calls it the first approval agreement under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act, for solar and battery projects in NSW: a project would be approved once by the NSW Government against state and federal standards, and once in place it would complement an assessment bilateral still being finalised. The agreement is not in force; DCCEEW’s page says comments close at 5pm AEDT on 11 January 2027.

Tenders

  • CIS Tender 12 opens for up to 5 GW of renewable generation in the NEM, with NSW projects excluded
    Source: Assistant energy minister; DCCEEW; ASL · 2026-11-27 · Verification: Verified · Ministerial release · Open tenders · ASL tender · RenewEconomy
    Key point: On 27 November Assistant Minister Josh Wilson said Capacity Investment Scheme Tender 12 had opened for up to 5 GW of renewable generation in the National Electricity Market, which the ASL tender page describes as an indicative target that excludes projects located in NSW. It is the first NEM generation tender to require the Developer Rating Scheme, so a proponent must have started that assessment before bidding, and it sets aside 500 MW for bids committing to at least 5% First Nations equity and/or equivalent revenue sharing. Registration closes at 5pm AEDT on 22 January 2027 and bids at 5pm AEDT on 5 February, with awards expected in June 2027; no projects have been awarded, and the minister said the government will then assess whether a further generation tender is needed in 2027.

Gas & firming

  • Domestic gas reservation passes Parliament: exporters must supply up to 20% of export gas to Australia
    Source: Resources minister; Climate and energy minister; Department of Industry; ABC News · 2026-11-27 · Verification: Verified · Joint release · Department notice · ABC
    Key point: On 27 November, Resources Minister Madeleine King, Climate Change and Energy Minister Chris Bowen and Industry Minister Tim Ayres said the Domestic Gas Reservation Scheme had passed Parliament, and the Department of Industry said the Australian Energy Regulator will administer it. LNG exporters must supply up to 20% of their gas exports to the Australian market, with export licensing from 1 January 2027 and the domestic supply obligation from 1 January 2028; Bowen said the scheme matters for the cost of living and for the energy transition, because gas firms renewable energy while solar and wind are built out. The 27 November ministerial release and department notice do not say whether Western Australia remains under its own policy, while ABC’s 26 November report said the state would keep the reservation policy it has run for two decades; that exemption is not in the 27 November official summaries.

Climate & power pathway

  • Annual climate statement and 2026 projections: the baseline puts NEM renewable generation at 84% in 2030
    Source: DCCEEW; Climate Change Authority; ABC News · 2026-11-26 · Verification: Verified · Projections · Projections PDF · Annual statement · CCA · ABC
    Key point: On 26 November DCCEEW published the fifth Annual Climate Change Statement and the 2026 emissions projections, and the Climate Change Authority published its progress advice the same day. National emissions fell by 6.2 million tonnes, and the CCA put renewables at around 44% of on-grid electricity, while ABC reported a 45.9% renewable share of National Electricity Market generation, a figure the department summary does not repeat. The baseline assumes current policies, including 82% renewable generation in national on-grid electricity by 2030, and on-time delivery of announced transmission; it projects NEM renewable generation at 84% in 2030 and 92% in 2040, with 2030 emissions 42% below 2005 and 0.3% above the 2021–2030 emissions budget, and 2035 emissions 48% below 2005 against a 62–70% target; these are scenario results, not a generation share already achieved.

Investment & certificates

  • CER full quarterly report: large renewable investment hits a record, with 13.8 GWh of batteries reaching FID
    Source: Clean Energy Regulator; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-26 · Verification: Verified · CER report · RenewEconomy
    Key point: On 26 November the Clean Energy Regulator published the full Quarterly Carbon Market Report for the September quarter of 2026: 2.1 GW of large-scale renewable generation reached final investment decision, including 1.1 GW of wind, the highest wind quarter the regulator has recorded, and 1.0 GW of solar. The total to the end of the quarter was 5.0 GW, already above the 2018 full-year record of 4.9 GW, and the report says further investment had taken the year-to-date total to 5.5 GW by mid-November. About 52% of generation capacity reaching FID through the end of the quarter had Capacity Investment Scheme support, the 2026–2027 FID projection is now 8–16 GW after the floor was raised from 6 GW, and battery storage reaching FID was 13.8 GWh in the quarter and 20.0 GWh in 2026; LRET approvals for 2026 were revised from 3.5–4.5 GW to 2.6–3.4 GW, and, citing AEMO, the report says greater grid-scale battery participation and lower demand from the grid helped NEM wholesale prices fall 27% from the September quarter of 2025.

Grid & Transmission

  • NSW appoints Transgrid to deliver the Hunter Transmission Project, and Downer puts the line contract at about A$1.1 billion
    Source: EnergyCo; Transgrid; Downer · 2026-11-23; 2026-11-24 · Verification: Verified · EnergyCo · Transgrid · Downer
    Key point: EnergyCo said on 23 November that the NSW Government has appointed Transgrid to deliver the 110 km, 500 kV Hunter Transmission Project, to move generation and storage from the Central-West Orana and New England renewable energy zones to load centres and to form part of a 500 kV ring. Planning approval was granted in July 2026 with 115 conditions, about 90% of the corridor is on mining, energy or government land, affected private properties were cut from 70 to fewer than 20, and construction is expected to support about 900 jobs. On 24 November Downer said EnergyCo had awarded it the line contract, working with Transgrid, at about A$1.1 billion over three years for the double-circuit line and towers from Bayswater South to Olney; that figure is the contractor’s package price, not a total project cost, and site mobilisation in 2027 remains subject to final approvals.

State policy

  • Victorian Coalition costings propose selling the SEC’s wind and battery assets, with the election on Saturday
    Source: ABC News; RenewEconomy; SEC · 2026-11-27 · Verification: Verified · ABC · RenewEconomy · Melbourne hub · Horsham · Delburn
    Key point: Victoria votes on 28 November, and the Liberal–National Coalition costings released by Opposition Leader Jess Wilson on 26 November propose selling the State Electricity Commission’s Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub, Horsham renewable energy park and Delburn Wind Farm, and ending further general-government funding for the SEC’s ongoing operations. SEC pages put the operating Plumpton hub at 600 MW / 1.6 GWh, co-owned with GreenPoint Energy; Horsham at 119 MW of solar already built plus a 100 MW, two-hour battery still being installed, with commercial operation expected in 2027; and Delburn at 33 turbines and 205 MW, which the SEC describes as fully publicly owned, under construction and expected in 2028. These are election commitments, not completed sales; ABC’s $13.8 billion figure is the Parliamentary Budget Office costing of a wider package that also includes sewage plants, roads and land, and RenewEconomy quotes Wilson putting an asset-recycling program at $12.6 billion, so neither figure is a sale price for the three energy assets alone.

Project pipeline

  • TagEnergy stops development of the Hillview hub in NSW and the Pa rooga wind and battery project in Queensland
    Source: TagEnergy; The Northern Daily Leader; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-27 · Verification: Verified · Project notice · Hillview · Pa rooga · Northern Daily Leader · RenewEconomy
    Key point: TagEnergy Australia posted a notice that, after a detailed evaluation, it has discontinued the Hillview Energy Hub in New South Wales and the Pa rooga wind farm and battery in Queensland. The Hillview page still lists 250–300 MW of solar, 200–300 MW of wind and a 1,000 MW battery of up to 8,000 MWh east of Uralla; the Northern Daily Leader on 27 November quoted Andrew Riggs, a managing partner for Australia, saying the wind resource could not offset the build cost, that inflation was 4.9% and the Walcha line’s timing was uncertain, and the paper described the battery as 1,000 MWh, which does not match the project page, so both accounts are kept. The Pa rooga page still describes an 800–1,040 MW wind farm with a co-located battery, south of Prairie, intended to connect to the proposed CopperString line; neither project page has been rewritten to say development has stopped, and the paper said Hillview was stopped while the environmental impact statement was being prepared, before construction.

Electricity demand

  • National EPA finds the Western Downs data centre needs no further federal assessment; referral cites about 1.44 GW of IT capacity
    Source: ABC News; EPBC referral summary · 2026-11-26 · Verification: Single source · ABC · Southern Queensland briefing · Referral summary · Deadline notice
    Key point: ABC reported on 26 November that the National Environmental Protection Agency determined the proposed Western Downs Digital Park near Dalby, Queensland, was unlikely to have a significant impact on matters of national environmental significance, so it does not need further federal environmental assessment. The referral summary describes four data-centre buildings with a combined information-technology capacity of about 1.44 GW, a permanent high-voltage connection to an existing Powerlink substation and battery-backed power conditioning; 1.44 GW is IT capacity in the referral, not a confirmed grid demand, and council and state approvals are still required. ABC describes the developer as Zerra DC, says the project is backed by Anthropic and puts the investment at about $32 billion, while the proponent on the referral is Western Downs Digital Park Asset Nominee 1 Pty Ltd; the decision outcome has been seen only in ABC’s report and is marked single source.
