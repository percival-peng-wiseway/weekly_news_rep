Policy & Regulation

AEMO opens consultation on reliability forecasting guidelines, closing 22 December
Source: AEMO | Date: 2026-11-24 | Verification: Verified
URL: Consultation
Key point: On 24 November AEMO published a consultation paper reviewing NEM reliability forecasting methods under the rules consultation procedure. It covers the ESOO and reliability forecast methodology, the EAAP guidelines, the Reliability Standard Implementation Guidelines, the Generation Information Guidelines and the MT PASA process description. The paper focuses on how energy limits are represented, how generator information is collected, and uncertainty about delivery of new projects. Submissions are due by 6:00 pm AEDT on 22 December 2026 to energy.forecasting@aemo.com.au. A draft report is scheduled for 19 February 2027 and a final report for 22 April 2027. The consultation meets requirements such as the four-year RSIG review. It does not itself change a reliability outcome.

Battery storage

Squadron’s Cullen Bullen battery enters the NSW planning system at 1,000 MW / 8,000 MWh
Source: Squadron Energy; NSW Planning | Date: 2026-11-24 | Verification: Verified
URL: Squadron · Planning Portal
Key point: Squadron Energy said on 24 November that the scoping report for the Cullen Bullen battery is on the NSW Planning Portal. The portal lists it as SSD-10485, at the Prepare SEARs stage, with Squadron Renewable Energy Developments Pty Ltd as applicant. The proposal is a 1,000 MW battery of up to eight hours (8,000 MWh), connecting by a double-line cut-in to an existing 500 kV line at 141 Red Springs Road, about 25 km north of Lithgow. The scoping report is dated 6 November 2026. The site is about 660 hectares and the preliminary development footprint about 26 hectares. The project is still seeking environmental assessment requirements. It is not on public exhibition and has no financing or construction decision.

Grid & Transmission

Downer discloses a Hunter line contract worth about A$1.1 billion
Source: Downer | Date: 2026-11-24 | Verification: Verified
URL: Downer announcement
Key point: Downer said on 24 November that EnergyCo has awarded it a contract, working with Transgrid, to build the transmission line for the Hunter Transmission Project. Downer put the value at about A$1.1 billion over a three-year construction period. The scope is about 110 km of double-circuit 500 kV line and lattice towers from the Bayswater South 500 kV switching station to the Olney 500 kV switching station. Design and procurement are expected to start in December 2026, with site mobilisation in calendar 2027, subject to final approvals. This is the contractor’s stated price for its own package. It is not Transgrid’s future AER revenue proposal, and it is not a total project cost in EnergyCo’s 23 November announcement.

Electric vehicles

Canberra’s The Griffin apartments get smart EV charging, with ARENA funding the national rollout
Source: energy.gov.au | Date: 2026-11-24 | Verification: Verified
URL: energy.gov.au
Key point: energy.gov.au reported on 24 November that residents of The Griffin in Canberra, a building of more than 250 apartments, can now charge EVs from NOX Energy smart wall sockets. Nearly 40% of residents have opted in. Residents reimburse the owners corporation for the energy they use, and the management system keeps charging inside the building’s existing electrical limits, avoiding a major upgrade. The national rollout covers up to 16 strata buildings; NOX has installed more than 750 of 2,000 sockets. ARENA’s Driving the Nation Program provided A$1.51 million toward the A$4.78 million rollout. A March rollout at Melbourne’s Sierra Hawthorn delivered more than 250 charging points and was, at the time, Australia’s largest apartment EV retrofit.

Markets & technology

BNEF scenarios put Australian solar at 137–215 GW by 2050 and short of 82% renewables in 2030
Source: pv magazine | Date: 2026-11-24 | Verification: Single source
URL: pv magazine
Key point: pv magazine reported on 24 November on BloombergNEF’s New Energy Outlook Australia. Under the economic transition scenario, with no further policy, solar grows from 43 GW in 2025 to 137 GW in 2050. Under the net-zero scenario, which decarbonises power, transport, industry and buildings by 2050, it reaches 215 GW. Onshore wind grows from 15 GW to 61 GW or 107 GW, and stationary batteries from 5 GW to 45 GW or 50 GW. Renewable generation in 2030 is about 61% in the economic transition scenario and 64% in the net-zero scenario. The net-zero case reaches 82% in 2035; the economic transition case is about 78% in 2035. Demand in 2050 is 339 TWh under the economic transition scenario, about 50% above 2025, and 506 TWh under net zero. These are model scenarios, not an official forecast and not a decision to drop the target. Single source.
Vestas says Australia is the lead market for its hybrid wind product path
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-24 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: In a 24 November RenewEconomy interview, Vestas senior vice president for onshore wind Juan Furones called Australia a top-three market, with nearly 800 local staff and almost 9 GW of wind installed, and described its grid conditions as among the most challenging worldwide. Vestas said it has been involved in about 11 hybrid projects over the past decade. Australian projects total about 1.2 GW, including Kennedy Energy Park and Port Augusta; another seven include solar and five include batteries, with about 450 MW of battery capacity. Furones also said that in the last week of the third quarter of 2026, Vestas-supplied projects reaching notice to proceed or start of construction totalled 958 MW. These are statements from the company interview. Single source.
