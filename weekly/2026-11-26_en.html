Climate & power pathway

Annual climate statement and 2026 projections: the baseline puts NEM renewable generation at 84% in 2030
Source: DCCEEW; Climate Change Authority; ABC News | Date: 2026-11-26 | Verification: Verified
URL: Projections · Projections PDF · Annual statement · CCA · ABC
Key point: On 26 November DCCEEW published Australia’s fifth Annual Climate Change Statement and the 2026 emissions projections. The Climate Change Authority published its 2026 Annual Progress Advice the same day. The statement page says national emissions fell by 6.2 million tonnes, about one and a half times the annual average over the previous decade, with the electricity sector accounting for the largest share. More than 622,000 home batteries have been installed under the Cheaper Home Batteries Program since July 2025. ABC reported a 1.3% fall, or 6.2 million tonnes, in the year to June 2026, a 3.6% fall in electricity emissions, and a record 45.9% renewable share of power in the National Electricity Market. Those percentages are ABC’s account of the statement. The department summary states the 6.2 million tonne fall and does not repeat 1.3%, 3.6% or 45.9%. The CCA page says emissions fell by around 6 million tonnes in the 12 months to June 2026, while staying on track for the 2030 and 2035 targets would require cuts of about 25 to 30 million tonnes a year. It puts renewables at around 44% of on-grid electricity, up from 24% in 2020, and says enough renewable projects have been approved to reach 82% by 2030 and now need to be built. The projections’ baseline assumes current policies are delivered, including the target of 82% renewable generation in national on-grid electricity by 2030. Under that assumption, emissions in 2030 are 42% below 2005 and 0.3% above the 2021–2030 emissions budget; they are 48% below 2005 in 2035 and 55% below in 2040. Electricity emissions are projected to fall 61%, from 142 Mt CO2-e in 2026 to 55 Mt in 2030. Renewable generation in the NEM is projected to reach 84% in 2030 and 92% in 2040, both assuming announced large transmission projects are delivered on time. A delay sensitivity, in which the NEM reaches 82% renewable generation only in 2032, puts 2030 emissions 26 Mt CO2-e above the baseline, with a difference of less than 1 Mt in 2035 and 2040. These are scenario results, not a generation share already achieved. The statement page also says that, under current policies, emissions in 2035 are projected at 48% below 2005, against a 2035 target of 62–70%.

Investment & certificates

CER full quarterly report: large renewable investment hits a record, with 13.8 GWh of batteries reaching FID
Source: Clean Energy Regulator; RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-26 | Verification: Verified
URL: CER report · RenewEconomy
Key point: The Clean Energy Regulator published the full Quarterly Carbon Market Report for the September quarter of 2026 on 26 November. The version history records version 1.0 dated 26/11/2026. A data workbook was released on 15 October; this document is the full report with analysis. In the quarter, 2.1 GW of large-scale renewable generation reached final investment decision, including 1.1 GW of wind, the highest quarter the regulator has recorded for that technology, and 1.0 GW of solar. The total to the end of the quarter was 5.0 GW, already above the previous full-year record of 4.9 GW set in 2018. The report says further investment had taken the year-to-date total to 5.5 GW by mid-November. About 52% of the generation capacity reaching FID through the end of the quarter had Capacity Investment Scheme support. The CER raised the lower bound of its 2026–2027 FID projection from 6 GW to 8 GW, so the range is now 8–16 GW. Approvals under the Large-scale Renewable Energy Target remain subdued: 0.6 GW was approved in the quarter and 2.1 GW year to date. With 570 MW under assessment on 30 September, the 2026 approvals projection was revised from 3.5–4.5 GW to 2.6–3.4 GW. A new large-battery tracker shows 13.8 GWh of storage reaching FID in the quarter and 20.0 GWh across 2026. The average capacity of batteries coming into service rose from 265 MWh in 2025 to 621 MWh in 2026. Citing AEMO, the report says greater grid-scale battery participation, together with lower demand from the grid, contributed to National Electricity Market wholesale prices falling 27% from the September quarter of 2025. Rooftop solar installations were 890 MW in the quarter, the strongest September quarter on record, taking 2026 to 2.8 GW and putting the year on track to exceed the previous annual record of 3.2 GW. Under Cheaper Home Batteries, 10.3 GWh had been installed and approved by the end of the quarter, and the full-year projection was raised from 8–12 GWh to 13–15.5 GWh. Preliminary Safeguard Mechanism data put covered emissions for 2025–26 at 133.3 Mt CO2-e, up from 132.8 Mt the year before. Emissions from facilities already covered the previous year fell 0.5%. The increase came mainly from large oil and gas facilities newly covered. Forty-eight facilities were below their baselines and may be eligible for about 4.5 million Safeguard Mechanism credits. One hundred and forty-five facilities were above their baselines, with 19.7 Mt of potential excess. Final data are due by 15 April 2027, and the preliminary figures can still change. The generic ACCU spot price was $38.40 at the end of the quarter and the SMC spot price was $39.25. The report says the ACCU spot price was $37.98 on 13 November.

Approvals

Commonwealth and NSW open consultation on an approval bilateral for solar and batteries, closing 11 January
Source: Federal environment minister; DCCEEW | Date: 2026-11-26 | Verification: Verified
URL: Joint release · DCCEEW page
Key point: On 26 November, Environment and Water Minister Murray Watt, NSW Minister for Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Heritage Penny Sharpe, and NSW Minister for Planning and Public Spaces Paul Scully opened public consultation on a proposed pilot approval bilateral agreement. The release says it would be the first approval agreement under the Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act, aimed at solar and battery projects in NSW. A project would be approved once by the NSW Government against state and federal standards, rather than through separate Commonwealth and state approvals. Once in place, it would complement the assessment bilateral now being finalised. The agreement is not in force. DCCEEW’s NSW bilateral page, updated the same day, confirms consultation on the draft approval bilateral is open until 5pm AEDT on 11 January 2027. The release asks for comments on the department’s Have Your Say website.

Electricity demand

National EPA finds the Western Downs data centre needs no further federal assessment; referral cites about 1.44 GW of IT capacity
Source: ABC News; EPBC referral summary | Date: 2026-11-26 | Verification: Single source
URL: ABC · Southern Queensland briefing · Referral summary · Deadline notice
Key point: ABC reported on 26 November that the National Environmental Protection Agency determined the proposed Western Downs Digital Park near Dalby, Queensland, was unlikely to have a significant impact on matters of national environmental significance, so it does not need further federal environmental assessment. ABC’s Southern Queensland briefing the same day said the decision was made the day before. The EPBC portal lists referral 2026/10667 as “Referral Decision Made” and does not show a readable decision date. A 29 October suspension notice set the revised statutory deadline at 25 November 2026. Council and state approvals are still required. The referral summary says the project would be built in four stages, with four data-centre buildings and a combined information-technology capacity of about 1.44 GW, a permanent high-voltage connection to an existing Powerlink substation, and battery-backed power conditioning. The 1.44 GW figure is IT capacity in the referral, not a confirmed grid demand. ABC describes the developer as Zerra DC, says the project is backed by Anthropic, and puts the investment at about $32 billion. The proponent named on the referral is Western Downs Digital Park Asset Nominee 1 Pty Ltd. The decision outcome has been seen only in ABC’s report. Single source.
