Regulation & compliance

Clean Energy Regulator declares a South Australian installer ineligible for the small-scale scheme for five months
Source: CER | Date: 2026-11-18 | Verification: Verified
URL: Media release · Signed declaration
Key point: On 18 November the Clean Energy Regulator published a media release declaring South Australian accredited installer David Mogg ineligible, under regulation 47 of the Renewable Energy (Electricity) Regulations 2001, to install solar PV or batteries under the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme for five months. Systems he installs while the declaration is in effect cannot receive small-scale technology certificates. The signed declaration is dated 16 November 2026 and runs until 16 April 2027. It follows adverse findings on three inspection reports, meaning one or more aspects of an installation did not meet Australian standards or scheme requirements. The declaration is separate from any action an accreditation body or a state or territory may take. CER chair Tony Chappel said that since 1 July 2025 the regulator has contacted more than 830 installers about adverse inspections, including 718 first advisory letters and 121 warning letters for repeated non-compliance. The 16 November briefing already covered the inspection-rate findings; this item is the named declaration, not a new set of inspection ratios.

Edify refers the 800 MW Glenarbon solar farm and four-hour battery in Queensland under the EPBC Act
Source: pv magazine Australia; RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-18 | Verification: Verified
URL: pv magazine · RenewEconomy
Key point: pv magazine Australia and RenewEconomy both reported on 18 November that Edify Energy has referred the Glenarbon solar and battery project for assessment under the federal EPBC Act. Both describe 800 MW of solar and an 800 MW, four-hour (3,200 MWh) battery near the Queensland–New South Wales border, about 65 km east of Goondiwindi, connecting to an existing Powerlink 330 kV line that crosses the site. pv magazine quoted Edify as saying the project could generate up to 1,555,000 MWh a year and that Edify expects it will not be a controlled action if mitigation is applied; that report puts the site at about 1,500 hectares, largely cleared and used for grazing. RenewEconomy puts the footprint at about 2,000 hectares and says the assessment refers to clearing about 3.1 hectares of potential grey-headed flying-fox habitat, about three years and 600 workers to build, and 12 other utility-scale renewable projects within 100 km. The two accounts of site area differ, and both are kept here. This is a referral, not an approval or an investment decision.
AGL says the first 100 units are in place at the Tomago battery in the Hunter
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-18 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 18 November that AGL had that day marked the installation of the first 100 battery units, out of more than 400, at the Tomago battery beside the Tomago aluminium smelter in the NSW Hunter. The project is 500 MW and four hours, or 2,000 MWh, and the report puts the cost at A$800 million. AGL construction general manager Johan Myburgh said it remains on schedule for completion in late 2027. Foundations are in, substation work is well advanced, and the first of two main transformers arrived the previous week. Fluence is the main supplier and builder. The NSW government awarded underwriting through a Long-Term Energy Service Agreement in May, and site work had already started in December 2025. The report says more than 200 people are working on construction. RenewEconomy’s comparison with the roughly A$750 million, 1,000 MWh Liddell battery is the outlet’s comparison, not a new cost announced in this progress update. This is a construction milestone, not commissioning. Single source.

Community solar

South Australia’s StrataCharge page offers shared solar and batteries grants and zero-interest loans of up to A$47,500 each
Source: Government of South Australia; RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-18 | Verification: Verified
URL: StrataCharge · RenewEconomy
Key point: The South Australian government’s StrataCharge page says eligible strata communities can get a grant of up to 50% of the cost of a shared solar and battery system, capped at A$47,500, and an optional zero-interest loan for the remaining cost, also capped at A$47,500, with applications handled by project partner Brighte Capital. The page says the program is co-funded through the Australian Government’s Community Solar Banks program. RenewEconomy reported on 18 November that the federal and South Australian governments announced the package over the weekend, putting the total at A$17.8 million, of which A$8.9 million is the federal Community Solar Banks contribution, with the loans coming through the CEFC Household Energy Upgrades Fund, and citing about 160,000 households in strata and community title. Those dollar totals and the household count are not on the government page checked for this briefing; they, and the quotes from energy minister Tom Koutsantonis and federal energy minister Chris Bowen, come from that report. This is a live support program, not an approval of an individual project.
