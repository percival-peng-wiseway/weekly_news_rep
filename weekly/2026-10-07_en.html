Policy & Regulation

Solar recycling inquiry weighs collection fees and changing metal content
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: RenewEconomy’s 7 October report examines differing evidence to the federal solar-panel reuse and recycling inquiry. RMIT and Livium warned that lower valuable-metal content could weaken recycling economics, while Pan Pacific offered a different assessment. Participants argued for fees to support collection and recycling; these are inquiry views, including evidence from August, rather than an enacted mandatory charge.

Solar

UNSW reports 23.5% certified perovskite submodule efficiency
Source: UNSW Sydney | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: On 7 October, UNSW reported that its collaboration with UtmoLight achieved certified stabilised efficiency of 23.5% for a 30 × 30 cm perovskite submodule, with a 676 cm² aperture. This improves the previous benchmark by 0.6 percentage points. The result is a research milestone, with further work needed on reproducibility, stability and manufacturing scale-up.

Battery Storage (BESS)

e-STORAGE selected for Muswellbrook’s 100 MW / 200 MWh battery
Source: pv magazine Australia / Canadian Solar | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: In reporting published on 7 October, pv magazine Australia detailed e-STORAGE’s agreement to supply and commission OX2’s 100 MW / 200 MWh Muswellbrook battery in the NSW Hunter Valley, supported by a 20-year service agreement. Canadian Solar’s original announcement is dated 6 October; shipments are planned for Q3 2027 and commercial operation for Q3 2028. The battery is not yet operational.
Leonora hybrid power project completes installation of 3,780 solar panels
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: pv magazine Australia reported on 7 October that all 3,780 panels had been installed at Leonora’s WA hybrid project, comprising 2.4 MW of solar, 9.9 MWh of batteries and 2.7 MW of diesel generation. Pacific Energy is delivering it under a 20-year power purchase agreement, with construction completion expected in early 2027 and first power during 2027; the design targets up to 60% renewable supply. The article reports an earlier construction milestone, not commissioning that day.

Wind

Neoen launches construction of 346 MW Goyder North Stage 1
Source: Neoen | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: Neoen announced on 7 October that notices to proceed had been issued to Vestas and Downer for the 346 MW Goyder North Stage 1 wind farm near Burra, South Australia. It will use 48 V172-7.2 MW turbines and is expected to operate in 2029. Wind output and the 454 MW / 1,814 MWh Goyder Battery support a 100 MW BHP baseload agreement; this announcement launches wind construction, rather than construction of the entire battery.
414 MW Uungula wind farm enters AEMO’s grid-management system
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 7 October that Squadron Energy’s 414 MW Uungula wind farm in NSW had entered AEMO’s grid-management system. This is a registration milestone preceding energisation, first generation and commissioning, rather than commercial operation. The report expects completion in mid-2027.

Company & Project Developments

Tandegin proposes 310 MW wind farm and large battery
Source: RenewEconomy / Collgar | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: RenewEconomy’s 7 October report describes the proposed Tandegin project near Merredin, WA: 310 MW of wind and approximately 300 MW / 1,800 MWh of battery storage. Collgar confirms up to 50 turbines and an in-development status; construction is hoped to start in late 2027, subject to approval. Planning consultation predates this article, so the report does not represent an approval or construction start on 7 October.

Infrastructure & Transmission

More than A$1.74 billion backs NSW road corridors serving renewable-energy logistics
Source: NSW Government / RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-10-07
URL: Original article
Key point: On 7 October, NSW announced road-corridor investment of A$520.8 million for Newcastle–Dubbo, A$849.8 million for Muswellbrook–Armidale and A$375 million for Euston–Tocumwal. RenewEconomy identifies A$1.285 billion as additional funding and links the routes to transporting equipment into renewable-energy zones. The programme funds roads and heavy-vehicle facilities, not electricity transmission lines.
