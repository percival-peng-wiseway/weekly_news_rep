Policy & Regulation

WA sets 75% renewable generation target for SWIS by 2030–31
Source: Western Australian Government | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: WA’s 27 October Plan for Affordable Energy sets a target of 75% renewable generation on the South West Interconnected System by the 2030–31 financial year. Its A$150 million Made in WA Energy Affordability Investment Program opened applications that day, offering manufacturers low-interest loans of up to A$15 million for solar, batteries and equipment upgrades. The generation share is a future policy target, not an achieved outcome; the finance is lending, not a grant.

Solar

Victoria widens apartment solar eligibility to buildings with up to 150 lots
Source: RenewEconomy / Victorian Government | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: RenewEconomy’s 27 October coverage follows the Victorian Government’s 26 October announcement expanding Solar for Apartments. Eligible buildings can have up to 150 lots, up from 50; the median property-value cap is removed and up to 20% of generation may serve common property. Rebates are capped at A$2,800 per apartment and A$140,000 per building, with applications open until June 2027. Funding requires an approved application, rather than being an automatic entitlement.

Battery Storage

Sigenergy launches 401 kWh C&I cabinet; Australian grid-connected release awaits listing
Source: pv magazine Australia / Sigenergy | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: Reporting and a company release on 27 October confirmed Sigenergy’s SigenCube C&I launch. The ES401 cabinet offers 401 kWh with integrated power conversion and energy management. The company says up to 100 cabinets can operate in parallel, with Australian off-grid and New Zealand availability planned from January 2027. Australian grid-connected availability will follow CEC listing. These are launch specifications and manufacturer claims, not independent performance verification or confirmation of current grid-connected approval.
Hazelwood Stage 2 details grid-forming battery configuration for 2028 target
Source: Sungrow / ENGIE | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: Sungrow’s 27 October release details 66 PowerTitan 2.0 battery units, 33 PCS units and an EMS3000 controller for Victoria’s 150 MW/300 MWh Hazelwood Stage 2 expansion. Sungrow supplies technology and Elecnor delivers balance-of-plant works, with commercial operation targeted for 2028. ENGIE announced financial close on 16 October: the fresh disclosure concerns equipment and grid-forming capabilities, not a new financial close or an operating battery.

Wind

Reported A$2 billion-plus Golden Plains Stage 2 deal remains unconfirmed
Source: RenewEconomy, citing AFR Street Talk | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 27 October, citing AFR Street Talk, that Octopus Investments had reportedly agreed to buy TagEnergy’s 577 MW Golden Plains Stage 2 in Victoria for more than A$2 billion. Neither party commented when approached, and the second stage was still being commissioned. This is an unconfirmed transaction report, not evidence that ownership transfer has completed or all capacity is commercially operational.

Companies & Projects

SEC reports Wurundjeri benefit-sharing agreement for Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub
Source: SEC Victoria | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source
Key point: SEC reported on 27 October that it had signed partnership and benefit-sharing agreements with the Wurundjeri Woi-wurrung Aboriginal Cultural Heritage Corporation covering the Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub battery, which SEC co-owns. The signing ceremony took place on 16 October. The co-designed agreements disclose no payment amount or share, and should not be described as a new equity transfer.

Grid & Related Infrastructure

Kimberley shared solar reaches homes without panels; reported totals differ
Source: RenewEconomy / Horizon Power | Date: 2026-10-27
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: RenewEconomy on 27 October attributed figures of 495 households and about A$275,000 in credits to Horizon Power, covering six Kimberley communities and about 1.5 MW on 183 rooftops. Horizon Power’s published release instead lists around 480 households and more than A$240,000, and dates final installations to August, not October. The differing figures and timing are retained rather than treated as a new commissioning event. This scheme shares solar credits; it is distinct from the national three-hour free-power offer.
