Tenders

CIS Tender 12 opens for up to 5 GW of renewable generation in the NEM, with NSW projects excluded
Source: Assistant energy minister; DCCEEW; ASL | Date: 2026-11-27 | Verification: Verified
URL: Ministerial release · Open tenders · ASL tender · RenewEconomy
Key point: On 27 November, Assistant Minister for Climate Change and Energy Josh Wilson said bids had opened that day under Capacity Investment Scheme Tender 12, for up to 5 GW of renewable generation in the National Electricity Market. The ASL tender page describes 5 GW as an indicative target and says projects located in NSW cannot take part. The release points those projects to future NSW Roadmap tenders. This is the first NEM generation tender to require the Developer Rating Scheme. A proponent must have started the assessment before bidding, shown by a signed and returned letter of engagement with the scheme provider. The tender sets aside 500 MW for bids that commit to at least 5% First Nations equity and/or an equivalent revenue-sharing arrangement and meet the qualification rules in the tender guidelines. Registration closes at 5pm AEDT on 22 January 2027, questions at 5pm AEDT on 29 January, and bids at 5pm AEDT on 5 February. Successful bids are due to be announced in June 2027. The minister said the application window is about 10 weeks, to allow for the end-of-year holidays. Results from Tender 9, the NEM generation tender already under way, are expected in early December 2026. The minister said that after Tender 12 the government will assess whether another generation tender is needed in 2027 to meet the scheme’s renewable capacity targets. ASL says the government may decide whether a further 2027 tender is required by comparing capacity under executed Capacity Investment Scheme Agreements with the generation target. The tender has opened. No projects have been awarded.

Gas & firming

Domestic gas reservation passes Parliament: exporters must supply up to 20% of export gas to Australia
Source: Resources minister; Climate and energy minister; Department of Industry; ABC News | Date: 2026-11-27 | Verification: Verified
URL: Joint release · Department notice · ABC
Key point: On 27 November, Resources Minister Madeleine King, Climate Change and Energy Minister Chris Bowen and Industry Minister Tim Ayres said the Domestic Gas Reservation Scheme had passed Parliament. The Department of Industry, Science and Resources said the same day that the Domestic Gas Reservation Bill 2026 and related bills passed Parliament on 27 November, and that the Australian Energy Regulator will administer the scheme. The joint release says LNG exporters will be required to supply up to 20% of their gas exports to the Australian market. The export licensing framework starts on 1 January 2027. The domestic supply obligation starts on 1 January 2028, so that it lines up with industry contracting cycles. The release says Parliament made amendments and does not list them. Bowen said the scheme matters for the cost of living and for the energy transition, because gas firms renewable energy while solar and wind are built out. On the evening of 26 November, ABC reported that the Senate had passed the bill that night and that the domestic supply requirement would take effect from 2028. The department dates passage through Parliament as 27 November. The 27 November ministerial release and department notice do not say whether Western Australia remains under its own reservation policy. ABC’s 26 November report said the Commonwealth and Western Australia had agreed an exemption so the state would keep the reservation policy it has run for two decades. That exemption is not in the 27 November official summaries. The supply obligation itself does not start until 2028.

State policy

Victorian Coalition costings propose selling the SEC’s wind and battery assets, with the election on Saturday
Source: ABC News; RenewEconomy; SEC | Date: 2026-11-27 | Verification: Verified
URL: ABC · RenewEconomy · Melbourne hub · Horsham · Delburn
Key point: Victoria votes on 28 November. Opposition Leader Jess Wilson released the Liberal–National Coalition’s election costings on 26 November. ABC set out the spending and savings on 27 November. ABC says the proposed sales include the Delburn wind farm and large-scale battery storage at Horsham and Plumpton. RenewEconomy reported the same day that the costing documents say an elected Coalition government would sell the State Electricity Commission’s three renewable assets — the Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub, the Horsham renewable energy park and Delburn Wind Farm — and return net proceeds and unspent asset funding to the general government sector. The documents also say the SEC would not receive further funding from the general government sector to cover ongoing operations. SEC project pages put the operating Melbourne Renewable Energy Hub at Plumpton at 600 MW / 1.6 GWh, with a $245 million SEC investment, co-owned with GreenPoint Energy. The Horsham park is a 119 MW solar farm plus a 100 MW, two-hour battery, with $370 million of SEC investment. The solar farm is built and the battery is still being installed, with commercial operation expected in 2027. Delburn is 33 turbines and 205 MW, under construction, expected in 2028. The SEC describes it as a fully publicly owned utility-scale wind farm, with anticipated capital expenditure of $600–700 million. These are election commitments, not completed sales. ABC says the Parliamentary Budget Office costed the wider asset package, which also includes sewage plants, roads and land, at $13.8 billion of savings over four years. That figure is not a sale price for the three energy assets alone. RenewEconomy quotes a Thursday statement from Wilson that put the asset-recycling program at $12.6 billion. The two figures are not the same measure, and both are left as reported. ABC said about two million people had already voted by Friday.

Project pipeline

TagEnergy stops development of the Hillview hub in NSW and the Pa rooga wind and battery project in Queensland
Source: TagEnergy; The Northern Daily Leader; RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-27 | Verification: Verified
URL: Project notice · Hillview · Pa rooga · Northern Daily Leader · RenewEconomy
Key point: TagEnergy Australia posted a notice on its project list saying that, after a detailed evaluation, it has discontinued the Hillview Energy Hub in New South Wales and the Pa rooga wind farm and battery in Queensland. The notice does not state capacity. The Hillview project page still describes a proposal east of Uralla, in the New England Renewable Energy Zone: 250–300 MW of solar, 200–300 MW of wind, and a battery of 1,000 MW / up to 8,000 MWh. On 27 November the Northern Daily Leader quoted Andrew Riggs, a managing partner for Australia. He said about four years had gone into measuring the wind and developing the project, and that the wind resource was not good enough to offset the cost of building it. He also said inflation was running at 4.9%, that the timing of the Walcha transmission line was uncertain, and that TagEnergy had tried to pass the project to other developers and found no buyers. The paper said the project was stopped while the environmental impact statement was being prepared, and that construction had not started. The paper described 250–300 MW each of wind and solar and a 1,000 MWh battery. That battery figure does not match the project page’s 1,000 MW / up to 8,000 MWh. Both accounts are kept here. The newspaper figure is not substituted for the company page. The Pa rooga page still describes an 800–1,040 MW wind farm with a co-located battery, south of Prairie in Flinders Shire, intended to connect to the proposed CopperString line. It does not give the battery’s megawatt-hours. Neither project page has been rewritten to say development has stopped. That statement is in the update on the project list.

Market rules

AEMO’s final decision sets a $0/MWh direct cost for intervention compensation of single-DUID hybrid plant
Source: AEMO | Date: 2026-11-27 | Verification: Verified
URL: Consultation · Draft report
Key point: On 27 November AEMO published the final documents closing its expedited consultation on the Methodology for AEMO Intervention Event Compensation. The amendment takes effect the same day. The consultation opened on 16 October to set how intervention compensation is calculated for a hybrid system behind a single dispatchable unit identifier. That kind of system registers more than one technology, such as solar and a battery, as one scheduled bidirectional unit. Written comments were due by 5pm Melbourne time on 13 November. AEMO said none were received, and that nobody asked within 10 business days of the draft report to use the standard consultation procedure instead. The final determination adopts the amendment in the mark-up. The 16 October draft says that where more than one technology or fuel sits behind a single DUID, the direct cost used for upfront compensation of an affected production unit is $0/MWh, in line with the battery short-run marginal cost benchmark in the market-suspension compensation methodology. The draft also says the zero value applies only to that upfront direct-cost calculation. It is not a finding that the participant had no compensable cost. A participant can still claim additional compensation under the National Electricity Rules. AEMO has also published a fact sheet on registration and operation of DC-coupled, single-DUID hybrid systems, and is preparing a webinar on it from 10:30am to 12pm AEDT on 10 December.
