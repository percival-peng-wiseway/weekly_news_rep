Policy & Regulation

NSW rule update gives customers more flexibility when joining virtual power plants
Source: NSW Climate and Energy Action | Date: 2026-10-12
URL：Original article
Key point: NSW published administrative changes to the Peak Demand Reduction Scheme (PDRS), effective 12 October, to give customers more flexibility when joining virtual power plants (VPPs). For BESS2 certificate creation, the October rule specifies nomination of the relevant capacity holder within 90 days of implementation and use of the most recently nominated holder at certificate creation. The substantive apartment and business battery incentives were introduced in July.
Data-centre clean-energy obligations remain under policy design
Source: RenewEconomy / Climate Council and partner organisations | Date: 2026-10-12
URL：Original article
Key point: In a 12 October interview, AEMC chair Anna Collyer said temporary use of Renewable Electricity Guarantee of Origin (REGO) certificates from existing generators, followed by catch-up obligations using new projects, remained a proposal for consideration; Commonwealth legislation would determine its duration. A same-day coalition statement, including the Climate Council, called for pipeline data centres to bring additional clean energy within three years of operation. These are policy proposals and stakeholder demands, rather than newly implemented requirements.

Solar

NEM rooftop solar reaches a new instantaneous high of 17,488 MW
Source: RenewEconomy / Global Power Energy | Date: 2026-10-12
URL：Original article
Key point: Reporting published on 12 October records National Electricity Market (NEM) rooftop PV output of 17,488 MW at 12:00 AEST on 11 October (13:00 Melbourne AEDT). This exceeded the 20 September record of 16,993 MW by 495 MW, or about 2.91%. It is an instantaneous generation record, with the event occurring a day before publication; previously covered state records are not repeated.

Company & Project Developments

EPA opens Warradarge solar-and-battery referral consultation until 18 October
Source: WA Environmental Protection Authority | Date: 2026-10-12
URL：Original article
Key point: WA’s EPA opened referral consultation on 12 October, closing 18 October, on whether to assess the Warradarge solar-and-battery proposal and at what assessment level. The previously reported project proposes up to 500 MW of solar and up to 500 MW of batteries within the existing wind farm, connecting to the SWIS through its existing 330 kV substation. The new milestone is consultation, not approval; battery energy capacity in MWh is not specified.

Infrastructure & Transmission

Climateworks modelling identifies savings from coordinated industrial energy planning
Source: Climateworks Centre | Date: 2026-10-12
URL：Original article
Key point: Climateworks Centre released modelling on 12 October using Gladstone as a case study. Sharing renewable generation, storage and supporting infrastructure, alongside coordinated energy use, could reduce infrastructure costs by up to 20% versus an uncoordinated transition and electricity prices by up to 50% versus Queensland’s current energy plans. It recommends industrial precinct energy plans to coordinate generation, storage and transmission; the savings are modelled outcomes, not realised results or approved investments.
