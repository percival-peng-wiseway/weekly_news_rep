Policy & Regulation

WA develops revenue support mechanism for new wind and solar
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source
Key point: RenewEconomy’s 28 October report quotes WA energy co-ordinator Jai Thomas outlining a Renewable Energy Mechanism still under development. It would provide annual payments when average market returns fall below the level needed by an efficient plant, based on actual generation rather than curtailed energy. Participating projects could also have their Reserve Capacity Price fixed for up to 15 years. These are fresh design details following the previously reported 75% renewables target, not an operating subsidy or awarded contracts.
WA consults on legalising plug-in solar and batteries
Source: ABC News / Western Australian Government | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: ABC reported on 28 October that WA was consulting on a safety pathway for plug-in solar and batteries. The government proposal allows up to 2 kW DC of panels and an 800 W AC inverter, with no upper storage-capacity limit. Feedback closes at 5 pm AWST on 9 December. Regulatory changes and a technical standard are still proposed, not confirmation that products are already legal to deploy. The government page is marked first published 20 September and updated 28 October; this item is anchored to the dated ABC report.
Victorian Opposition reiterates transmission review without firm deadline
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source
Key point: At All Energy Australia on 28 October, Opposition energy spokesperson David Davis reiterated that a Coalition government would independently review Victoria’s transmission plan, while acknowledging renewables as the largest component of new generation. RenewEconomy reports that he made no new policy announcements and gave no firm completion deadline. This is a conditional election position, not a decision already changing or cancelling current transmission works. The unresolved review timetable remains relevant to project investment planning.

Solar

SMA unveils DC-coupled grid-forming solar-plus-storage solution
Source: pv magazine Australia / SMA Australia | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: SMA Australia unveiled a DC-coupled solar, battery and grid-forming package at All Energy Australia on 28 October. The company says it can capture otherwise clipped solar generation and provide synthetic inertia, fast frequency response and voltage support. This is a technology unveiling, not a new plant entering service; connection approval, performance and economics still require project-specific assessment.

Battery Storage

CATL selected for 400 MW/1.86 GWh Baranduda battery
Source: RenewEconomy / pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: Reporting on 28 October quoted Birdwood Energy confirming CATL as battery supplier for the planned 400 MW/1.86 GWh Baranduda project near Wodonga, Victoria, equivalent to about 4.65 hours of storage. The new milestone is supplier selection, not commissioning. CATL also showcased its 9 MWh TENER+ and sodium-ion offering. Industry interest is not an Australian sodium-battery order, and does not establish that Baranduda will use sodium-ion technology.

Companies & Projects

Fortescue signs first external Green Grid customer for planned 2029 supply
Source: RenewEconomy / Fortescue | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 28 October that Fortescue had secured PLS Group as its first external Green Grid customer under a 20-year agreement for at least 325 GWh a year at the Pilgangoora lithium operation, with supply expected from 2029. Fortescue will build an approximately 25 km, 220 kV connection; PLS will fund it and own 20 km. The company release is dated 27 October. This is a signed supply agreement and planned connection, not commencement of power delivery. The Green Grid combines wind, solar and battery storage.

Grid & Related Infrastructure

ARENA backs Alchemy Charge apartment charging programme with A$2 million
Source: The Driven / Energy Source & Distribution | Date: 2026-10-28
URL: Original source · Supporting source
Key point: Two Australian outlets reported on 28 October that Alchemy Charge secured A$2 million in ARENA co-funding for its A$7.4 million Strata Assist programme. A free digital platform is scheduled for 1 February 2027 to support apartment charging approvals, quotations and installation workflows, alongside individually metered, load-managed sockets. Origin will operate charging at participating buildings. Installations remain subject to electrical assessment; switchboard upgrades cannot be ruled out for every site.
