Markets

IEEFA and Green Energy Markets: 2.4 GW committed to construction in the September quarter, a record
Source: IEEFA; RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-19 | Verification: Verified
URL: IEEFA · RenewEconomy
Key point: On 19 November IEEFA and Green Energy Markets released the Q3 2026 Australian Renewable Energy Scorecard. Large renewable projects committed to construction from July to September totalled 2.4 GW, 20% above the previous record in Q1 2022, of which 1.5 GW was wind. The scorecard says Queensland now has more than 90% of the wind turbines under construction that it needs to bridge the gap to its 2030 Integrated System Plan requirement, and that Western Australia and South Australia have closed 60% of the gap to their 2030 wind requirements. On storage, utility batteries in construction in the June quarter equalled 21% of the new capacity needed by 2030, and commitments this quarter added a further 13 percentage points. RenewEconomy quotes the scorecard as putting batteries, wind and solar at 34%, 21% and 16% of the extra capacity needed by 2030. The authors’ estimate that, at this quarter’s rate, the remaining utility-battery gap could be in construction in less than a year and a half is a projection, not a locked schedule. Projects already in construction represent about A$8 billion and 21,600 job-years; if the remaining fully approved renewable and large-battery projects proceed, the scorecard estimates about A$51 billion and 142,500 job-years. The scorecard also says capacity now under construction is still significantly below what must be operating by 2030. RenewEconomy further quotes the scorecard that only about 6% of fully approved projects are also contracted, and that NSW needs about 17.5 GW of new wind and solar. Small-scale battery installations fell 44% in the quarter and installation size fell 20%; RenewEconomy says average size is still more than double the average before the July 2025 subsidy. Tristan Edis of Green Energy Markets attributes the Queensland and WA progress largely to state-government support for power purchase agreements, including the previous Queensland government’s support for Rio Tinto’s Gladstone smelter to contract renewable energy. This is a quarterly tally and the authors’ judgement, not a new government target.

Grid connection

AEMO publishes an interim guideline for connecting inverter-based loads, with comments due 17 December
Source: AEMO | Date: 2026-11-19 | Verification: Verified
URL: Consultation
Key point: On 19 November AEMO published an Interim Guideline for Connection of Inverter Based Loads, and a FAQ, covering connection of inverter-based loads, including data centres, to the National Electricity Market. It is the interim approach while technical guidelines are finished after the AEMC’s Improving the NEM access standards – Package 2 rule change. Requirements are meant to reflect a project’s size, location and impact on power system security. Feedback is asked for by 17 December 2026 to contact.connections@aemo.com.au; AEMO says feedback after that date is still welcome, with the next update planned for early 2027. A webinar is set for 2:00pm on Wednesday 2 December; AEMO’s page gives the time as AEST. This is a connection-guidance consultation. It does not change projects that are already approved.

System operations

AEMO: storm damage brings down two Eyre Peninsula transmission towers, with no broader NEM impact at that stage
Source: AEMO | Date: 2026-11-19 | Verification: Verified
URL: Media statement
Key point: In a statement at 7:00pm AEDT on 19 November, AEMO said two transmission towers supplying South Australia’s Eyre Peninsula were down because of storm damage. Transmission owner ElectraNet had confirmed the damage, which caused local power outages. AEMO said that, at that stage, there was no broader impact on power system security and reliability in the National Electricity Market, and that it would keep monitoring. The statement does not give a customer count and does not ask households to switch off rooftop solar. Reporting the next day gave a different tower count and more specific operating instructions; this item records only the statement issued that evening.

Companies & projects

pv magazine: EDF Australia acquires the proposed Holloway battery in the New England REZ
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-19 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: pv magazine Australia reported on 19 November that EDF power solutions Australia has acquired the proposed Holloway battery from Australian developer SynCo Global. The report says the project is 250 MW, near Armidale in New South Wales, about 650 metres from the Armidale substation in the New England Renewable Energy Zone, on a site of about 3 hectares and about 380 km north-west of Sydney. Planning and environmental assessment are under way and an environmental impact statement is imminent. A final investment decision on the state-significant development is targeted for late 2027, with operations targeted for 2029. EDF said the battery is anticipated to operate for up to 30 years and then be decommissioned, with components removed and recycled where possible. The headline describes the project as grid-forming, but the article text does not set out grid-forming capability or a megawatt-hour capacity, so neither is treated here as a confirmed fact. This is a project acquisition, not a construction start or a final investment decision. Single source.
Report: Sonnen Australia is in voluntary administration, with a creditors’ meeting on 30 October
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-19 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 19 November that Sonnen Australia, the local arm of the German home-battery maker, is in voluntary administration. The report says an ASIC notice was published nearly a month earlier appointing Hugh Armenis and Jason Lloyd Porter of SV Partners Adelaide as joint administrators, and that the first creditors’ meeting was on 30 October. Administration therefore did not start on 19 November; that is the day this outlet reported it. The report also says this followed Shell’s sale of the company by less than three months, that the German business has filed for insolvency, and that Enervision, which had been supporting Australian customers, has ended its arrangement with Sonnen. Who stands behind warranties, software and spare parts is described as still unanswered. The report relays a Clean Energy Council reply to a customer that responsibility is most likely to fall to installers and retailers, depending on the warranty text. This briefing has not checked the ASIC notice. Single source.
