Policy & Regulation

Guarantee of Origin scheme expands product-emissions certification
Source: DCCEEW / Assistant Minister for Climate Change and Energy | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: On 13 October the Commonwealth announced expanded Guarantee of Origin (GO) certification for aluminium, iron ore, biogas, biomethane and additional hydrogen production pathways. GO records product-related emissions and supports renewable-electricity certification; eligibility does not itself make every hydrogen pathway renewable or certify products against a specific low-emissions threshold.
NSW sets 1,500 MW firming target and opens tender-design consultation
Source: ASL / NSW Climate and Energy Action | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: ASL’s 13 October plan targets 1,500 MW of additional NSW firming capacity operational by end-May 2033, with sustained supply for at least eight hours. Tender and contract-design consultation closes 4 November; procurement is expected in early 2027. NSW expects gas peakers to supply most capacity, although other technologies may qualify; contracts have not yet been awarded.

Solar

ARENA commits up to A$31.1 million for Australian solar-robot manufacturing
Source: Australian Renewable Energy Agency (ARENA) | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: ARENA announced up to A$17.8 million for Luminous Robotics’ Melbourne manufacturing facility, supporting around 100 panel-installation robots by 2029, and up to A$13.3 million for Built Robotics’ Brisbane hub and 12 autonomous piling systems. The 13 October Solar Sunshot Round 2 funding advances demonstrated technology towards local commercial-scale manufacturing; these are planned deployments.

Battery Storage (BESS)

Darnum’s 200 MW / 800 MWh battery enters federal referral consultation
Source: RenewEconomy / EPBC Act Public Portal | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: Reporting on 13 October confirms Samsung C&T Renewable Energy Australia’s Darnum battery entered EPBC referral consultation on 12 October, with comments closing 26 October. The proposed 200 MW / 800 MWh project at Shady Creek, Victoria, would connect to AusNet’s 220 kV line. It remains under development and approval review; this federal step is distinct from the previously covered state planning consultation.
Blackstone clears federal referral step; final storage configuration remains unclear
Source: RenewEconomy / EPBC Act Public Portal | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 13 October that Octopus Australia’s proposed Queensland Blackstone battery received an EPBC not-controlled-action outcome; the decision date was not established from the accessible primary record. The referral describes 500 MW / 2,000 MWh, while the developer’s website and earlier state approval still show 1,000 MWh. Final configuration remains unclear, and this environmental milestone does not establish financial close or construction commencement.

Wind

Huxley Hill refurbishment extends five turbines’ lives by at least ten years
Source: Tasmanian Government | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: Tasmania announced on 13 October that the Huxley Hill wind-farm upgrade was complete, extending five turbines’ working lives by at least ten years and returning an early-2025 storm-damaged turbine to operation. The refurbished assets support King Island’s isolated grid alongside solar, batteries and diesel generation; the announcement does not specify the physical completion date.

Infrastructure & Transmission

Tribunal affirms Marinus Link’s Heybridge converter-station permit
Source: Tasmanian Government | Date: 2026-10-13
URL：Original article
Key point: Tasmania’s Civil and Administrative Tribunal affirmed the planning permit for Marinus Link’s Heybridge Converter Station on 13 October, following an earlier ruling on the separate North West Transmission Developments permit. This is a new planning milestone beyond previously reported licence and works announcements, rather than the interconnector entering service.
