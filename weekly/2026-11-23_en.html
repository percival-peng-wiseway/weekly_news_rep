Policy & Regulation

Industry working group recommends three standard electricity contracts for the proposed ESEM
Source: ASL | Date: 2026-11-23 | Verification: Verified
URL: ASL release
Key point: The industry working group convened by DCCEEW and ASL, with the AER as an observer, published its stage-one report on 23 November. It recommends three long-term standard contracts for the proposed Electricity Services Entry Mechanism (ESEM): a Regional Reference PPA for bulk energy, a Daily Dynamic Block Spread swap for shaping, and a $600/MWh cap for firming. Energy ministers have agreed in principle, except Queensland, to the core recommendations of the 2025 NEM Review, including an ESEM. Long-form contracts, ministerial decisions and designation by the ESEM administrator are still outstanding. This is a design recommendation, not an open tender.
Nexa says an average 5.8 GW of east-coast coal capacity was unavailable in 2025
Source: Nexa Advisory | Date: 2026-11-23 | Verification: Single source
URL: Report PDF · RenewEconomy
Key point: Consultancy Nexa Advisory’s Coal Closure Certainty report, which RenewEconomy reported as released on 23 November, says average unavailable NEM coal capacity was 7.3 GW in 2022, 5.8 GW in 2023, 5.3 GW in 2024 and 5.8 GW in 2025. Even in the best of those years, about a quarter of the coal fleet was unavailable on average. In 2025, Gladstone unit 1 was unavailable for 67.5% of potential capacity-hours, and Callide C3, Eraring 4, Tarong North 1, Vales Point 5, Yallourn 2 and Tarong 1 were each around 47–50%. Nexa says high coal unavailability coincides with more high-price intervals when residual demand is high: Victoria rose from 10.7 to 53.0 intervals per 10,000, and Queensland from 77.3 to 107.5. A call for nationally coordinated closure dates is Nexa’s recommendation, not a government decision. The article reports the same study, so this is marked single source.

Grid & Transmission

NSW appoints Transgrid to deliver and operate the Hunter Transmission Project
Source: EnergyCo; Transgrid | Date: 2026-11-23 | Verification: Verified
URL: EnergyCo · Transgrid
Key point: EnergyCo said on 23 November that the NSW Government has appointed Transgrid to deliver the Hunter Transmission Project. The 110 km, 500 kV line is intended to move generation and storage from the Central-West Orana and New England renewable energy zones to load centres and to form part of a 500 kV ring. Planning approval was granted in July 2026 with 115 conditions. About 90% of the corridor is on mining, energy or government land, and affected private properties were cut from 70 in the preliminary corridor to fewer than 20. Design-and-construct contractors have also been appointed. Construction is expected to support about 900 jobs. The AER oversaw procurement. The construction-contract price is in Downer’s 24 November announcement and is not treated here as the total project cost.
Rooftop solar export ban continues on SA’s Eyre Peninsula to protect Port Lincoln diesel units
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-23 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 23 November that the ban on rooftop solar exports from about 10,000 homes and businesses on the southern Eyre Peninsula will last at least a few more days, after last Thursday’s storm brought down transmission towers installed in 2023. The ban is intended to protect the Port Lincoln diesel generators now supplying that part of the peninsula. Premises with small batteries may use solar on site and charge, but may not export. The islanded section has no big battery, and the 62 MW Cathedral Rocks wind farm is offline because of the line damage. ElectraNet’s Alex Trento said the towers were designed for winds up to about 170 km/h. Premier Malinauskas said the investigation would test whether the winds exceeded that design or the specification was at fault. Only this report was found.

Projects

pv magazine: APA starts building the Sybella Creek solar and battery project in north-west Queensland
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-23 | Verification: Single source
URL: pv magazine · APA project page
Key point: pv magazine Australia reported on 23 November that APA has started construction of Sybella Creek near Mount Isa, a A$259 million project with 72 MW of solar and a 52 MW/104 MWh battery. It will connect to the APA-operated North West Power System, which is not part of the NEM, and is described as that system’s first large battery. It sits beside the 88 MW Dugald River solar farm, is underpinned by a long-term energy supply agreement with Evolution Mining subsidiary Ernest Henry Mining, and has UGL as EPC contractor. The report says construction should finish in early 2028, with supply targeted around mid-2028. APA’s project page confirms the capacity and the mid-2028 supply target. The construction-start date rests on this report and is marked single source.

Carbon market

CEFC: Tiwi Islands carbon plantation beats its first-year planting target by more than 20%
Source: CEFC | Date: 2026-11-23 | Verification: Verified
URL: CEFC release
Key point: The CEFC said on 23 November that Tiwi-owned Tiwi Plantations has planted 1,798 hectares since January 2026, more than 20% above its first-year target, and sown more than two million seedlings. Tiwi employees were about 59% of the workforce, contributed more than 33,000 hours, earned more than A$1.4 million and completed 196 formal training sessions. ACCUs are expected from 2027, with about five million over the project life under the plantation-forestry method. The CEFC has committed A$40 million and River Capital A$41 million toward a planned 30,000-hectare estate. Midway manages the forestry and ACCU marketing. This is a natural-capital and carbon project, not a generation asset.
