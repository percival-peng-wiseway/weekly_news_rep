Policy & Regulation

NSW Bendemeer solar and storage project referred to the Independent Planning Commission after public objections
RenewEconomy · 2026-11-16 · Source
Key point: The solar-and-storage component of Metis Energy’s Bendemeer Renewable Energy Hub proposes 255 MW DC of solar and a 150 MW/300 MWh battery in NSW’s New England region. It was referred to the NSW Independent Planning Commission (IPC) after 119 public objections. The daily briefing says NSW projects receiving 50 or more objections must be referred to the IPC.
AER consults on a trial waiver for AGL and Endeavour Energy’s FlexTogether proposal
AER · 2026-11-17 · Verification: Verified · Consultation
Key point: On 17 November the AER opened consultation on a trial waiver sought by AGL and Endeavour Energy through the Energy Innovation Toolkit. The application, lodged on 20 August, asks for a temporary exemption from NER clauses 8.6.1(b)(1), 8.6.1(b)(2) and 6.17.1 so the two parties can share data and use AGL customers’ batteries, electric vehicles and shifted consumption to address local issues on Endeavour’s distribution network. The proposal has two stages: matching trial locations and flexible resources, then testing procurement and delivery. This is a consultation, not a granted waiver. Submissions close on 22 January 2027 and go to regulatorysandbox@aer.gov.au. The proponents say they hope to form a commercial model and extend it to other networks and retailers after the trial. The daily briefing says that is the proponents’ stated intention, not an AER decision.

Clean Energy Regulator declares a South Australian installer ineligible for the small-scale scheme for five months
CER · 2026-11-18 · Verification: Verified · Media release · Signed declaration
Key point: On 18 November the Clean Energy Regulator published a media release declaring South Australian accredited installer David Mogg ineligible, under regulation 47 of the Renewable Energy (Electricity) Regulations 2001, to install solar PV or batteries under the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme for five months. Systems he installs while the declaration is in effect cannot receive small-scale technology certificates. The signed declaration is dated 16 November 2026 and runs until 16 April 2027. It follows adverse findings on three inspection reports, meaning one or more aspects of an installation did not meet Australian standards or scheme requirements. The declaration is separate from any action an accreditation body or a state or territory may take. CER chair Tony Chappel said that since 1 July 2025 the regulator has contacted more than 830 installers about adverse inspections, including 718 first advisory letters and 121 warning letters for repeated non-compliance. The 16 November briefing already covered the inspection-rate findings; this item is the named declaration, not a new set of inspection ratios.
South Australia’s StrataCharge page offers shared solar and battery grants and zero-interest loans of up to A$47,500 each
Government of South Australia; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-18 · Verification: Verified · StrataCharge · RenewEconomy
Key point: The South Australian government’s StrataCharge page says eligible strata communities can get a grant of up to 50% of the cost of a shared solar and battery system, capped at A$47,500, and an optional zero-interest loan for the remaining cost, also capped at A$47,500, with applications handled by project partner Brighte Capital. AEMO publishes an interim guideline for connecting inverter-based loads, with comments due 17 December
AEMO · 2026-11-19 · Verification: Verified · Consultation
Key point: On 19 November AEMO published an Interim Guideline for Connection of Inverter Based Loads, and a FAQ, covering connection of inverter-based loads, including data centres, to the National Electricity Market. It is the interim approach while technical guidelines are finished after the AEMC’s Improving the NEM access standards – Package 2 rule change. Requirements are meant to reflect a project’s size, location and impact on power system security. Feedback is asked for by 17 December 2026 to contact.connections@aemo.com.au. AEMO says feedback after that date is still welcome, with the next update planned for early 2027. A webinar is set for 2:00pm on Wednesday 2 December; AEMO’s page gives the time as AEST. This is a connection-guidance consultation. It does not change projects that are already approved.
AEMO consults on changes to the DER Register information guidelines, closing 15 January 2027
AEMO · 2026-11-20 · Verification: Verified · Consultation
Key point: On 20 November AEMO opened a rules consultation on amendments to the DER Register Information Guidelines and published a draft. The register was established in 2020 under National Electricity Rules clause 3.7E as standing data on the location, capacity and technical characteristics of distributed energy resources connected to the National Electricity Market. The consultation covers existing data fields and technical settings, standing data for active DER management, inverter serial numbers, validation and verification, the treatment of different DER system types, and workflow improvements. Written submissions are due by 5:00pm Melbourne time on 15 January 2027 to DERRegister@aemo.com.au. AEMO’s indicative timetable has a second-stage paper and draft determination expected on 26 February 2027, submissions on that stage expected by 2 April 2027, and a final report expected on 7 May 2027. Those later dates may change. This is a guidelines consultation. It does not change register obligations on the day.
Report: the COP31 draft drops the fossil-fuel transition and the Pacific resilience fund
RenewEconomy (AAP) · 2026-11-20 · Verification: Single source · Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 20 November, citing AAP, that the United Nations released a shortened draft of the COP31 cover decision late on Thursday Australian time, cut from 18 pages to nine. The draft removes references to a faster transition away from fossil fuels, net zero by 2030 and net-negative emissions by 2040 for developed countries, adaptation finance of at least US$120 billion by 2035, and the Pacific Resilience Facility championed by Australia. A target for electricity to reach 35% of global energy use by 2035, and a target to triple renewable energy capacity, remain in the draft. Chris Bowen, Australia’s climate change and energy minister and president of the negotiations, said the draft was written at the strongest level that consultations suggested could attract consensus, and that talks would continue until almost 200 countries agreed. The Australian Conservation Foundation and Greenpeace criticised the removal of the fossil-fuel language. This is a draft during the meeting, not an adopted decision. Single source.

Electricity market

IEEFA and Green Energy Markets: 2.4 GW committed to construction in the September quarter, a record
IEEFA; Green Energy Markets; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-19 · Verification: Verified · IEEFA · RenewEconomy
Key point: On 19 November IEEFA and Green Energy Markets released the Q3 2026 Australian Renewable Energy Scorecard. Large renewable projects committed to construction from July to September totalled 2.4 GW, 20% above the previous record in Q1 2022, of which 1.5 GW was wind. The scorecard says Queensland now has more than 90% of the wind turbines under construction that it needs to bridge the gap to its 2030 Integrated System Plan requirement, and that Western Australia and South Australia have closed 60% of the gap to their 2030 wind requirements. On storage, utility batteries in construction in the June quarter equalled 21% of the new capacity needed by 2030, and commitments this quarter added a further 13 percentage points. RenewEconomy quotes the scorecard as putting batteries, wind and solar at 34%, 21% and 16% of the extra capacity needed by 2030. Projects already in construction represent about A$8 billion and 21,600 job-years; if the remaining fully approved renewable and large-battery projects proceed, the scorecard estimates about A$51 billion and 142,500 job-years. The scorecard also says capacity now under construction is still significantly below what must be operating by 2030. RenewEconomy further quotes the scorecard that only about 6% of fully approved projects are also contracted, and that NSW needs about 17.5 GW of new wind and solar. Small-scale battery installations fell 44% in the quarter and installation size fell 20%; RenewEconomy says average size is still more than double the average before the July 2025 subsidy.
Authors’ judgement: The authors’ estimate that, at this quarter’s rate, the remaining utility-battery gap could be in construction in less than a year and a half is a projection, not a locked schedule. Tristan Edis of Green Energy Markets attributes the Queensland and Western Australia progress largely to state-government support for power purchase agreements, including the previous Queensland government’s support for Rio Tinto’s Gladstone smelter to contract renewable energy. The daily briefing says this is a quarterly tally and the authors’ judgement, not a new government target.
AEMO expects electricity and gas supplies to be sufficient this summer under forecast conditions
AEMO; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-20 · Verification: Verified · Media release · RenewEconomy
Key point: On 20 November AEMO published its 2026–27 summer readiness outlook. Michael Gatt, executive general manager of operations, said electricity and gas supplies are expected to remain sufficient under forecast conditions across Australia’s main energy systems, but conditions can change quickly in extreme heat. Since December 2025, three batteries totalling 600 MW have reached full output in Western Australia’s wholesale market, and more than 30 new projects totalling 8.4 GW have been added in the National Electricity Market, including 5.3 GW of batteries, 1.1 GW of solar, 0.7 GW of wind, 0.7 GW of gas and 0.5 GW of hybrid solar and batteries. The weather outlook is warmer and drier than average across most of Australia, with El Niño expected to persist into autumn 2027. Heatwaves in Queensland and New South Wales may be longer and more intense, while hot spells in the south may be more intense but generally shorter, and both markets could see new record maximum demand. Gatt also said rooftop solar can push daytime operational demand to very low levels, particularly on weekends and public holidays, which is a different operating problem from heatwave peaks. This is a seasonal preparedness assessment, not a forecast of blackouts.

Li-S Energy announces the first commercial sale of its Australian-made lithium-sulphur cells
ASX announcement; pv magazine Australia · 2026-11-17 · Verification: Verified · ASX announcement · pv magazine
Key point: Li-S Energy (ASX: LIS) told the market on 17 November that SOFWERX has bought its 10 Ah lithium-sulphur Power Cells on commercial terms, for evaluation on crewed and uncrewed air, land and maritime systems for US Special Operations Command. The company said this is its first commercial sale of cells, modest in scale, and an early commercial validation. Report: South Australia asks Port Lincoln rooftop solar to switch off after the town is islanded
RenewEconomy · 2026-11-20 · Verification: Single source · Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 20 November that about 24,000 properties lost power on South Australia’s Eyre Peninsula after the storm. Energy minister Tom Koutsantonis declared an electricity supply emergency at 6:15pm on Thursday. The report says local generation at about 9:45pm Thursday restored about 10,000 customers, mostly in Port Lincoln and surrounding towns, leaving that area islanded from the National Electricity Market. The minister asked people in the Port Lincoln region to switch off rooftop solar, where safe, so it would not trip the temporary generators, and said the request did not apply to the rest of the state. ElectraNet’s Friday update said some communities could remain without power for several days and again asked Port Lincoln customers to switch off inverters until advised otherwise, with temporary towers and then permanent repairs to follow. Alex Trento of ElectraNet told the ABC, as relayed by RenewEconomy, that the towers were built for a one-in-2,000-year weather event and that local winds appear to have exceeded that. This is a temporary operating measure after the outage, not a new standing export limit. Single source.
Figures that differ: AEMO’s 7:00pm statement on 19 November said two towers. This report cites a 6pm Thursday update from SA Power Networks saying three ElectraNet transmission towers between Port Lincoln and Cleve were out of service. The daily briefing says the counts do not match and keeps both.
