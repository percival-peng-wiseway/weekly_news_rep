Electricity market

AEMO expects electricity and gas supplies to be sufficient this summer under forecast conditions
Source: AEMO; RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-20 | Verification: Verified
URL: Media release · RenewEconomy
Key point: On 20 November AEMO published its 2026–27 summer readiness outlook. Michael Gatt, executive general manager of operations, said electricity and gas supplies are expected to remain sufficient under forecast conditions across Australia’s main energy systems, but conditions can change quickly in extreme heat. Since December 2025, three batteries totalling 600 MW have reached full output in Western Australia’s wholesale market, and more than 30 new projects totalling 8.4 GW have been added in the National Electricity Market, including 5.3 GW of batteries, 1.1 GW of solar, 0.7 GW of wind, 0.7 GW of gas and 0.5 GW of hybrid solar and batteries. The weather outlook is warmer and drier than average across most of Australia, with El Niño expected to persist into autumn 2027. Heatwaves in Queensland and New South Wales may be longer and more intense, while hot spells in the south may be more intense but generally shorter, and both markets could see new record maximum demand. Gatt also said rooftop solar can push daytime operational demand to very low levels, particularly on weekends and public holidays, which is a different operating problem from heatwave peaks. This is a seasonal preparedness assessment, not a forecast of blackouts.

Distributed energy

AEMO consults on changes to the DER Register information guidelines, closing 15 January 2027
Source: AEMO | Date: 2026-11-20 | Verification: Verified
URL: Consultation
Key point: On 20 November AEMO opened a rules consultation on amendments to the DER Register Information Guidelines and published a draft. The register was established in 2020 under National Electricity Rules clause 3.7E as standing data on the location, capacity and technical characteristics of distributed energy resources connected to the National Electricity Market. The consultation covers existing data fields and technical settings, standing data for active DER management, inverter serial numbers, validation and verification, the treatment of different DER system types, and workflow improvements. Written submissions are due by 5:00pm Melbourne time on 15 January 2027 to DERRegister@aemo.com.au. AEMO’s indicative timetable has a second-stage paper and draft determination expected on 26 February 2027, submissions on that stage expected by 2 April 2027, and a final report expected on 7 May 2027. Those later dates may change. This is a guidelines consultation. It does not change register obligations on the day.

System operations

Report: South Australia asks Port Lincoln rooftop solar to switch off after the town is islanded
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-20 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 20 November that about 24,000 properties lost power on South Australia’s Eyre Peninsula after the storm. It cited a 6pm Thursday update from SA Power Networks saying three ElectraNet transmission towers between Port Lincoln and Cleve were out of service. AEMO’s 7:00pm statement on 19 November said two towers. The counts do not match, and both are kept. Energy minister Tom Koutsantonis declared an electricity supply emergency at 6:15pm on Thursday. The report says local generation at about 9:45pm Thursday restored about 10,000 customers, mostly in Port Lincoln and surrounding towns, leaving that area islanded from the National Electricity Market. The minister asked people in the Port Lincoln region to switch off rooftop solar, where safe, so it would not trip the temporary generators, and said the request did not apply to the rest of the state. ElectraNet’s Friday update said some communities could remain without power for several days and again asked Port Lincoln customers to switch off inverters until advised otherwise, with temporary towers and then permanent repairs to follow. Alex Trento of ElectraNet told the ABC, as relayed by RenewEconomy, that the towers were built for a one-in-2,000-year weather event and that local winds appear to have exceeded that. This is a temporary operating measure after the outage, not a new standing export limit. Single source.

Climate negotiations

Report: the COP31 draft drops the fossil-fuel transition and the Pacific resilience fund
Source: RenewEconomy (AAP) | Date: 2026-11-20 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 20 November, citing AAP, that the United Nations released a shortened draft of the COP31 cover decision late on Thursday Australian time, cut from 18 pages to nine. The draft removes references to a faster transition away from fossil fuels, net zero by 2030 and net-negative emissions by 2040 for developed countries, adaptation finance of at least US$120 billion by 2035, and the Pacific Resilience Facility championed by Australia. A target for electricity to reach 35% of global energy use by 2035, and a target to triple renewable energy capacity, remain in the draft. Chris Bowen, Australia’s climate change and energy minister and president of the negotiations, said the draft was written at the strongest level that consultations suggested could attract consensus, and that talks would continue until almost 200 countries agreed. The Australian Conservation Foundation and Greenpeace criticised the removal of the fossil-fuel language. This is a draft during the meeting, not an adopted decision. Single source.
