Policy & regulation

AER consults on a trial waiver for AGL and Endeavour Energy’s FlexTogether proposal
Source: AER | Date: 2026-11-17 | Verification: Verified
URL: Consultation
Key point: On 17 November the AER opened consultation on a trial waiver sought by AGL and Endeavour Energy through the Energy Innovation Toolkit. The application, lodged on 20 August, asks for a temporary exemption from NER clauses 8.6.1(b)(1), 8.6.1(b)(2) and 6.17.1 so the two parties can share data and use AGL customers’ batteries, electric vehicles and shifted consumption to address local issues on Endeavour’s distribution network. The proposal has two stages: matching trial locations and flexible resources, then testing procurement and delivery. This is a consultation, not a granted waiver. Submissions close on 22 January 2027 and go to regulatorysandbox@aer.gov.au. Any later commercial model or expansion to other networks and retailers is the proponents’ stated intention, not an AER decision.
Report: Chalmers talks of MYEFO “fine balances” and does not announce a new home-battery decision
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-17 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 17 November that Treasurer Jim Chalmers told the ASIC Annual Forum in Sydney that morning the mid-year budget update would include “a substantial savings package”, would “cut enough, but not too much”, and would strike “fine balances” so already announced “responsible cost-of-living relief” could continue without adding to price pressures. The outlet reads that language as a hint that the Cheaper Home Batteries rebate may avoid a further major cut. That reading is the outlet’s inference, not a government decision on the rebate. The report also describes pressure from the opposition and some crossbenchers to cut flagship decarbonisation programs. This briefing has not checked a transcript of the speech. Single source.

Report: AGL’s Abermain battery at Ipswich was ruled not a controlled action last week
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-17 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 17 November that AGL’s Abermain battery at Tivoli in Ipswich, Queensland — 125 MW and up to about 500 MWh — was determined the previous week to be “not a controlled action” under the federal EPBC Act, so it does not require a full assessment. It would connect at Powerlink’s nearby Abermain substation; Powerlink is separately developing, and would own and maintain, the underground connection. Ipswich City Council granted development approval in late 2023. AGL bought the state-approved project from Firm Power in 2024. This is not a construction start or a final investment decision. The report also mentions Mount Britton (500 MW / 2,000 MWh) and Tuckeroo (500 MW and up to 2,000 MWh) as separate proposals still in the approvals queue, not part of this decision. This briefing has not opened the EPBC decision notice. Single source.

Battery manufacturing

Li-S Energy announces the first commercial sale of its Australian-made lithium-sulphur cells
Source: ASX announcement; pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-17 | Verification: Verified
URL: ASX announcement · pv magazine
Key point: Li-S Energy (ASX: LIS) told the market on 17 November that SOFWERX has bought its 10 Ah lithium-sulphur Power Cells on commercial terms, for evaluation on crewed and uncrewed air, land and maritime systems for US Special Operations Command. The company said this is its first commercial sale of cells, modest in scale, and an early commercial validation; further platform-specific batches are expected only if the evaluation is positive, and any larger purchase depends on those results and later procurement decisions. The company said the cells approach twice the energy density of conventional lithium-ion and use no graphite, nickel or cobalt. pv magazine Australia the same day quoted company figures of 1,700 W/kg continuous power density, 2,400 W/kg peak, and gravimetric energy density approaching 400 Wh/kg, and said manufacturing is at Geelong with headquarters in Brisbane. Those performance figures are company claims, not an independent test. This is not a volume supply contract.
