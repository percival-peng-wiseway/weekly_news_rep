Renewable Energy in AU

Jul 20 ~ 24, 2026 · Weekly Summary
Data Centre Mandate Grid Records & Transmission Project Pipeline Technology & Long-Duration Storage Governance & Integrity

1. Data Centres Face a "Net Generator" Mandate

The most consequential policy signal of the week came from Prime Minister Albanese, who unveiled plans to legislate a requirement that large-scale data centres become "net generators" — obligated to build their own new renewable generation and storage, inject no less power into the grid than they consume, and bear the full cost of grid connection. The proposal, to be advanced through a new AI Office housed within the Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, aims to head off the looming collision between explosive AI-driven electricity demand and Australia's decarbonisation targets.

Public sentiment is squarely behind the move: polling released mid-week showed Australians overwhelmingly want data centre operators to fund their own clean energy infrastructure rather than passing costs to households. Analysts argued the data centre boom need not spike power prices — provided governments unlock the pipeline of stalled renewable projects through faster transmission connections and streamlined approvals. Yet the Northern Territory threw a counterpunch, committing land for a gas-backed data centre that has drawn sharp criticism from environmental groups worried about fossil fuel lock-in.

2. Grid Hits Record Heights as Transmission Deals Unlock Gigawatts

AEMO's quarterly connections scorecard revealed that FY26 was a banner year: 34 projects reached full output, adding 9.1 GW of new generation and storage capacity — more than double the FY25 tally — along with 12.9 GWh of storage. Battery storage dominated the mix, while wind farm applications hit an all-time annual record of 12 projects totalling 5.2 GW.

On the transmission front, New South Wales sealed a landmark deal between EnergyCo and Transgrid to upgrade the Jerilderie–Wagga Wagga corridor, unlocking 3.56 GW of solar, wind, and storage in the South West Renewable Energy Zone. Transgrid also pivoted its system strength strategy, opening a pathway for up to 900 MW of grid-forming battery storage after synchronous condenser costs surged 38%. The AEMC pressed ahead with final rules to improve distribution network visibility of rooftop solar, home batteries, and EVs.

3. Project Pipeline Surges from Victoria to Queensland

The week was punctuated by major project milestones. Vestas installed the final turbine at Golden Plains in Victoria — at 1.33 GW, Australia's largest wind farm, set to supply roughly 9% of the state's electricity. RWE's 1.1 GW Theodore wind farm in Queensland cleared its final federal environmental hurdle, having already won state approval and a CIS tender, putting it on track for a final investment decision and 2030 operations.

In storage, Wärtsilä completed delivery of all 1,038 Quantum units to EnergyAustralia's 350 MW / 1,474 MWh Wooreen BESS in Victoria's Latrobe Valley, a critical step before the Yallourn coal plant retires in mid-2028. Naturgy committed over A$490 million to Queensland's largest solar-battery hybrid — 290 MW of PV plus 180 MW / 360 MWh of storage. Samsung C&T lodged a 250 MW / 1,000 MWh solar-plus-storage project near Goulburn, and Yancoal's former Stratford coal mine won planning consent for a 3.6 GWh pumped hydro and solar repurposing.

4. Technology Bets Shift Toward Long-Duration and Next-Generation Solar

The CSIRO's final GenCost 2025-26 report crystallised a trend reshaping investment decisions: pumped hydro costs are being revised upward while battery storage costs continue down. Queensland reportedly shelved the 2 GW / 20 GWh Mt Rawdon pumped hydro project, even as Idemitsu announced plans for an 8-hour vanadium flow battery in the NSW Hunter. In Victoria, the SEC partnered with Italy's Energy Dome to deploy a 20 MW compressed-CO₂ long-duration storage system.

On the solar frontier, Monash University researchers achieved an indium-free tandem solar cell at 31% efficiency. Australian perovskite startup Halocell struck a manufacturing partnership with a South Korean firm to scale flexible perovskite production. ARENA backed next-generation solar with A$95.4 million and funded a forestry-byproduct-to-sustainable-aviation-fuel project.

5. Governance Gaps: Enforcement, Delays, and a "Trust Problem"

Beneath the project headlines, tensions over governance ran through the week. The Clean Energy Regulator suspended 21 solar and battery installation companies for false statements — including fabricated installation volumes and forged compliance documents. The nation's Net Zero chief publicly rebuked an unnamed state government for falling short on emissions targets, calling it a "question of trust."

Planning delays emerged as a recurring industry grievance, with developers warning slow approvals are adding cost and complexity. Origin Energy disclosed an investigation into a potential data security breach, and analysts flagged the growing challenge of solar panel decommissioning driven by shifting economics rather than physical aging.
