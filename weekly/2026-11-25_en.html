Projects & approvals

Acen seeks federal environmental approval for the Belyando wind, solar and battery project near Moranbah
Source: RenewEconomy; pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-25 | Verification: Verified
URL: RenewEconomy · pv magazine
Key point: RenewEconomy and pv magazine Australia both reported on 25 November that Acen Australia has lodged an EPBC referral with DCCEEW for the Belyando Energy Project, about 25 km southwest of the coal town of Moranbah on roughly 1,670 hectares of grazing land. The proposal is up to 500 MW of solar, 440 MW of wind and a battery with a concept design of 500 MW / 1,600 MWh, close to the existing transmission network, with grazing expected to continue. RenewEconomy also reported up to about 55 turbines of about 8 MW each, and said the southern section was removed after koala and greater glider habitat was found. pv magazine said Acen has more than 1 GW of large-scale renewables in construction and operation and a development pipeline above 13 GW, including the completed 400 MW Stubbo solar farm, the New England solar farm being expanded from 400 MW to 720 MW, and a 200 MW / 400 MWh battery expected to be finished in the first half of 2027. The referral is in the approval process. It is not an approved project.
Townsville residents raise flood and wet-season access concerns about a Black River battery
Source: ABC News | Date: 2026-11-25 | Verification: Single source
URL: ABC
Key point: ABC’s North Queensland briefing on 25 November reported that resident Sue Shore, founder of the Stop Mill Road Black River BESS group, says a planned 200 MW battery on a neighbouring Black River property has not adequately considered flood risk. She said Alice River residents are cut off every wet season and that four battery projects are planned in the area, so emergency access would not be available year-round. Trina Solar said the project is at an early stage and a social impact assessment is underway. A council spokesperson said the council is engaging with Trina Solar on the assessment of the proposed Yabulu South BESS. The project is not approved. Single source.

Hydrogen

Envision and Greensteel Australia explore up to about 400 MW of green hydrogen for NSW steel
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-25 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 25 November that Envision has signed a strategic partnership with Australian company Greensteel Australia to assess a renewable-powered green hydrogen system of up to about 400 MW for lower-carbon iron and steel in NSW. Greensteel’s first stage is electric induction heating and forging at Mayfield, the former BHP steelworks site in Newcastle. A later stage would be hydrogen-based direct reduced iron and an electric arc furnace. A regional NSW site is still being assessed. The companies said industrial synergies, local manufacturing such as wind-tower components, and the technology choice depend on technical and commercial assessment and on approvals. This is a partnership to study the option, not a final investment decision. Single source.
Report says Siemens will build a digital twin for InterContinental’s hydrogen node
Source: RenewEconomy; ARENA project page | Date: 2026-11-25 | Verification: Single source
URL: RenewEconomy · ARENA project page
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 25 November that Siemens and Australian firm Mescada will build a digital twin of InterContinental Energy’s P2(H2)Node, so equipment configurations can be compared before FEED and a final investment decision. The report covers the Western Energy Hub and the Australian Renewable Energy Hub in the Pilbara. ARENA’s project page records the same Digital Twin Template, with A$1.56 million from the Advancing Renewables Program toward a A$3.12 million cost, running from 2 April 2026 to 29 January 2028. The page was last updated on 8 April 2026, so the funding is not a new grant announced on 25 November. Claims of a 10–20% cost cut from the node architecture, and up to a further 20% from the twin, are company statements. The new partnership detail is single source.

Households & curtailment

Solar Juice says home-battery stocks may exceed six months, with some sales below cost
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-25 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: RenewEconomy on 25 November quoted a LinkedIn post by Rami Fedda, co-founder of wholesaler Solar Juice. He said the Cheaper Home Batteries Program lifted battery attachment on solar jobs from about 20% to more than 100%, and that volumes in May fell about 80% from April after the policy change. He said supply-chain stocks may be about six months and that some distributors are selling more than 20% below cost. Nathan Avins of Tindo Solar and other industry voices said installer and retailer contraction has already started. Further tightening, an earlier end to the scheme, or a 14 kWh cap on eligible capacity are industry speculation. The government has not announced them. These are industry statements, not regulator statistics. Single source.
Commentary: renewable curtailment is rising, but some of the equipment to absorb it is already installed
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-25 | Verification: Single source
URL: Source
Key point: pv magazine Australia published a commentary by Andy Foster on 25 November. Figures he cites include 5,709 MW of renewable curtailment in Victoria around midday on 11 October 2026; 2.93 TWh of NEM wind and solar curtailment in the first half of this year, 37% more than a year earlier and about 7% of wind and solar output; and average network curtailment of 281 MW in AEMO’s September quarter, about double the year before. He also cites about 27 GW of rooftop solar across about 3.9 million systems, more than 600,000 home batteries under the Cheaper Home Batteries Program, and grid-scale batteries absorbing about 13 GWh during the day. Rystad, as cited, put utility-scale solar curtailment at 22% of available generation in September, down from 26% a year earlier. His argument is that storage and flexible demand already installed should be coordinated to absorb midday surplus before new transmission is built. This is one commentary. The figures are the author’s citations of AEMO and Rystad and were not re-checked here against the source datasets. Single source.
