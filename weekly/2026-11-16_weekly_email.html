电力市场

  • IEEFA 与 Green Energy Markets：三季度 2.4 GW 承诺开工，为纪录最高
    来源：IEEFA；Green Energy Markets；RenewEconomy · 2026-11-19 · 核验：已核验 · IEEFA · RenewEconomy
    要点：IEEFA 与 Green Energy Markets 11 月 19 日发布的三季度记分卡称，7 月至 9 月承诺开工的大型可再生项目为 2.4 GW，比 2022 年一季度前纪录高 20%，其中风电 1.5 GW。记分卡称昆州在建风机已超过补上其 2030 年综合系统计划风电缺口所需的 90%，西澳和南澳各自补上 60%；RenewEconomy 引记分卡称电池、风电和光伏分别达到 2030 年额外需求的 34%、21% 和 16%。记分卡同时写明，目前在建容量仍明显低于 2030 年必须投运的新增容量。
  • AEMO：预报条件下今夏电力和天然气供应预计仍然充足
    来源：AEMO；RenewEconomy · 2026-11-20 · 核验：已核验 · 新闻稿 · RenewEconomy
    要点：AEMO 11 月 20 日发布的 2026–27 年夏季准备展望称，预报条件下主要能源系统的电力和天然气供应预计仍然充足，但极端高温时可能很快变化。自 2025 年 12 月以来，西澳三座合计 600 MW 的电池达到满出力，国家电力市场新增 30 多个项目、合计 8.4 GW，其中电池 5.3 GW、光伏 1.1 GW、风电 0.7 GW、燃气 0.7 GW、光储混合 0.5 GW。这是季节准备评估，不是停电预报。

AU Renewable Energy Weekly: 16 Nov – 20 Nov 2026

Policy & Regulation

  • AER consults on a trial waiver for AGL and Endeavour Energy’s FlexTogether proposal
    Source: AER · 2026-11-17 · Verification: Verified · Consultation
    Key point: On 17 November the AER opened consultation on a trial waiver sought by AGL and Endeavour Energy. The application, lodged on 20 August, asks for a temporary exemption from NER clauses 8.6.1(b)(1), 8.6.1(b)(2) and 6.17.1 so the parties can share data and use household batteries, electric vehicles and shifted consumption on Endeavour’s distribution network. This is a consultation, not a granted waiver; submissions close on 22 January 2027 at regulatorysandbox@aer.gov.au.
  • AEMO publishes an interim guideline for connecting inverter-based loads, with comments due 17 December
    Source: AEMO · 2026-11-19 · Verification: Verified · Consultation
    Key point: On 19 November AEMO published an interim guideline for connecting inverter-based loads, including data centres, to the National Electricity Market, covering the period after the AEMC’s Improving the NEM access standards – Package 2 rule change. Comments are due by 17 December 2026 at contact.connections@aemo.com.au, and a webinar is set for 2:00pm on 2 December (AEMO’s page gives the time as AEST). This is a connection-guidance consultation and does not change projects that are already approved.

Electricity market

  • IEEFA and Green Energy Markets: 2.4 GW committed to construction in the September quarter, a record
    Source: IEEFA; Green Energy Markets; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-19 · Verification: Verified · IEEFA · RenewEconomy
    Key point: The Q3 2026 scorecard from IEEFA and Green Energy Markets, released on 19 November, says 2.4 GW of large renewable projects were committed to construction from July to September, 20% above the Q1 2022 record, of which 1.5 GW was wind. It says Queensland now has more than 90% of the wind turbines under construction needed to bridge its 2030 Integrated System Plan gap, and that Western Australia and South Australia have each closed 60%; RenewEconomy quotes the scorecard as putting batteries, wind and solar at 34%, 21% and 16% of extra capacity needed by 2030. The scorecard also says capacity now under construction is still significantly below what must be operating by 2030.
  • AEMO expects electricity and gas supplies to be sufficient this summer under forecast conditions
    Source: AEMO; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-20 · Verification: Verified · Media release · RenewEconomy
    Key point: AEMO’s 2026–27 summer readiness outlook, published on 20 November, says electricity and gas supplies are expected to remain sufficient under forecast conditions across the main energy systems, but conditions can change quickly in extreme heat. Since December 2025, three batteries totalling 600 MW have reached full output in Western Australia, and more than 30 new projects totalling 8.4 GW have been added in the National Electricity Market, including 5.3 GW of batteries, 1.1 GW of solar, 0.7 GW of wind, 0.7 GW of gas and 0.5 GW of hybrid solar and batteries. This is a seasonal preparedness assessment, not a forecast of blackouts.

Battery Storage (BESS)

  • Clean Energy Regulator finds 61.19% of inspected home batteries were substandard
    Source: pv magazine Australia · 2026-11-16 · Source
    Key point: pv magazine Australia reported that 61.19% of battery systems inspected by the Clean Energy Regulator in FY2025–26 were rated substandard and 0.95% unsafe, across 6,554 inspections. The regulator said the common issues were chiefly workmanship-related, and that it has contacted more than 830 installers about adverse results since 1 July 2025.
  • AGL says the first 100 units are in place at the Tomago battery in the Hunter
    Source: RenewEconomy · 2026-11-18 · Verification: Single source · Source
    Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 18 November that AGL had confirmed the first 100 battery units, out of more than 400, were in place at the Tomago battery beside the Tomago aluminium smelter in the NSW Hunter. The project is 500 MW and four hours, or 2,000 MWh; the report puts the cost at A$800 million, and AGL said it remains on schedule for completion in late 2027. This is a construction milestone, not commissioning, and the report is a single source.

Company & Project Developments

  • Frontier Energy begins construction of stage one of WA’s Waroona solar and battery project
    Source: pv magazine Australia · 2026-11-16 · Source
    Key point: Frontier Energy has started major construction on stage one of the Waroona Renewable Energy Project, about 120 km south of Perth, with 132 MW of solar and an 81.5 MW, 6.9-hour battery, and first generation scheduled for 2028. It will connect to the SWIS at the 330 kV Landwehr Terminal; the company’s longer-term target is about 1 GW of solar and 660 MW of storage by 2031.
  • Edify refers the 800 MW Glenarbon solar farm and four-hour battery in Queensland under the EPBC Act
    Source: pv magazine Australia; RenewEconomy · 2026-11-18 · Verification: Verified · pv magazine · RenewEconomy
    Key point: pv magazine Australia and RenewEconomy both reported on 18 November that Edify Energy has referred Glenarbon for federal EPBC assessment: 800 MW of solar and an 800 MW, four-hour (3,200 MWh) battery, about 65 km east of Goondiwindi, connecting to an existing Powerlink 330 kV line that crosses the site. The two accounts of site area differ (about 1,500 hectares and about 2,000 hectares), and both are kept. This is a referral, not an approval or an investment decision.

System operations

  • Report: South Australia asks Port Lincoln rooftop solar to switch off after the town is islanded
    Source: RenewEconomy · 2026-11-20 · Verification: Single source · Source
    Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 20 November that about 24,000 properties lost power on the Eyre Peninsula after the storm, that energy minister Tom Koutsantonis declared an electricity supply emergency at 6:15pm on Thursday, and that Port Lincoln was then islanded and asked to switch off rooftop solar where safe. AEMO’s statement on the evening of 19 November said two towers were down, while the SA Power Networks update cited in the report said three; the counts do not match and both are kept. This is a temporary operating measure, not a new standing export limit, and the report is a single source.
