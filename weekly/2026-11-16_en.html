Policy & Regulation

NSW Bendemeer solar and storage project referred to Independent Planning Commission after public objections
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-11-16
URL: Source
Key point: The solar-and-storage component of Metis Energy’s Bendemeer Renewable Energy Hub proposes 255 MW DC of solar and a 150 MW/300 MWh battery in NSW’s New England region. It was referred to the NSW Independent Planning Commission (IPC) after 119 public objections; NSW projects receiving 50 or more objections must be referred to the IPC.

Solar

Central Queensland Power seeks about 980,000 modules for Queensland’s Wooderson solar and battery project
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-16
URL: Source
Key point: Central Queensland Power has opened an expression-of-interest process for the Wooderson project at Mount Alma, about 40 km southwest of Gladstone. The proposal comprises 450 MW of solar, a 450 MW DC-coupled BESS with up to 3,600 MWh of storage, and about 980,000 PV modules; construction is targeted for Q1 2028 and completion for December 2030, subject to approvals, financing and investment decisions.

Battery Storage (BESS)

Clean Energy Regulator finds 61.19% of inspected home batteries were substandard
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-16
URL: Source
Key point: Of battery energy storage systems inspected under the Small-scale Renewable Energy Scheme in FY2025–26, 61.19% were rated substandard and 0.95% unsafe; the Clean Energy Regulator completed 6,554 inspections in the period. The regulator said common issues were chiefly workmanship-related and that it has contacted more than 830 installers over adverse inspection outcomes since 1 July 2025.

Company & Project Developments

Frontier Energy begins construction of stage one of WA’s Waroona solar and battery project
Source: pv magazine Australia | Date: 2026-11-16
URL: Source
Key point: Frontier Energy has started major construction on stage one of the Waroona Renewable Energy Project, about 120 km south of Perth, comprising 132 MW of solar and an 81.5 MW, 6.9-hour battery system, with first generation scheduled for 2028. It will connect to the SWIS via the 330 kV Landwehr Terminal; Frontier’s longer-term target is about 1 GW of solar and 660 MW of storage by 2031.
