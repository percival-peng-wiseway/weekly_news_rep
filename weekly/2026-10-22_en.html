Solar & Hybrid Projects

1414 Degrees orders 5B systems for Aurora’s initial 70 MW solar development
Source: RenewEconomy | Date: 2026-10-22
URL: Original source
Key point: RenewEconomy reported on 22 October that 1414 Degrees placed an initial order for 70 MW of 5B Maverick prefabricated solar systems for Aurora near Port Augusta, South Australia, with manufacturing planned in North Adelaide. The solar development forms part of Stage 1 alongside an approved 140 MW/280 MWh battery. Power purchase agreements, infrastructure partnerships and financing are still progressing. The precinct’s approximately 2 GW solar potential is indicative, not the size of this order or commissioned capacity.
Ampt selected for planned Victorian solar-and-storage hybrid
Source: Ampt / AAP | Date: 2026-10-22
URL: Original source
Key point: Ampt announced on 22 October that its string optimisers were selected for an unnamed planned Victorian hybrid comprising 8.2 MWdc of solar, 4.95 MWac of conversion capacity and 20 MWh of storage. Its Direct-to-Battery design connects PV to the shared DC bus between battery and inverter. The company claims equipment-cost and yield benefits and fast grid response. This is a technology selection, not commissioning; no CEFC financing award for this particular project was disclosed.

Battery Storage

SAJ announces HS5 battery-system approval ahead of All Energy Australia
Source: SAJ / PR Newswire | Date: 2026-10-22
URL: Original source
Key point: In a 22 October announcement, SAJ said its EK70 HS5 and EK90 HS5 integrated home-energy systems had received CEC approval under SA TS 5398, describing them as the first products approved under the standard. That first-product claim is company-supplied, not independently ranked here. SAJ plans to exhibit at All Energy Australia in Melbourne on 28–29 October, with certificate presentation on 28 October. The exhibition remains upcoming, and the precise approval date was not disclosed.

Wind & Mine Electrification

Fortescue reports first self-lifting wind-tower installation at Nullagine
Source: RenewEconomy / Fortescue | Date: 2026-10-22
URL: Original source · Quarterly report
Key point: Fortescue’s 22 October quarterly briefing described installation of the first three self-lifting towers at its 133 MW Nullagine wind project. Its quarterly report also confirms construction of 35 kilometres of 33 kV distribution lines at Christmas Creek, ongoing commissioning of two mining-equipment fast chargers at Eliwana, and 21 electric excavators operating. These developments support the Pilbara green grid and mine electrification. Tower installation and equipment deliveries do not mean the wind farm has entered commercial operation.

Infrastructure & Pumped Hydro

Snowy 2.0 targets A$22.2 billion cost and late-2029 commissioning
Source: Snowy Hydro | Date: 2026-10-22
URL: Original source
Key point: Snowy Hydro’s 22 October reassessment sets Snowy 2.0’s target cost at A$22.2 billion, with an allowance for an A$25 billion scenario, and targets full commissioning in late 2029. Machine tunnelling is 65% complete and cavern excavation 90% complete. Contractor oversight and cost controls are being strengthened, while the Commonwealth’s revised capital strategy is still being developed. These are revised estimates and targets for a project under construction, not a final bill or guaranteed operating date.
Alinta withdraws 900 MW Oven Mountain pumped-hydro proposal
Source: ABC News | Date: 2026-10-22
URL: Original source
Key point: ABC reported on 22 October that Alinta Energy had withdrawn its proposed 900 MW, A$1.8 billion Oven Mountain pumped-hydro scheme in NSW. The company cited economics and timing difficulties involving key elements, including transmission. The development was still undergoing planning assessment: this is a withdrawn proposal, not closure of an operating station. The article’s battery-cost comparisons are expert commentary, not a project-specific cost audit.
